# Kirk Hammett: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Notes per bar, bend sizes beyond the half step, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no article and stay [I]. Lesson examples are the writers' own licks "in the style"; only habits they state as Hammett's are kept.

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic is home: "frequent use of the minor pentatonic" [S, MR Play like]; "draws heavily on pentatonics", two-note-per-string shape 1 pull-offs on the top two strings [S, GW 10 techniques]; E minor pentatonic in solos [S, LL Through the Never]. His own reason: "the best scale for mimicking vocal melodies is the pentatonic"; "harder to say stuff with pentatonics" [S, GW Trolls].
- The blues scale's b5 worked hard, not just passed through: "a speedy repetitive lick that works the 'blue' note" [S, MR Play like]; "a fan of soloing with the blues scale" [S, GW 10 techniques]; "I'm a lot bluesier" now, "Hendrixisms that I always try to sneak in" [S, GW Prime Cuts, GW Load].
- Natural minor (Aeolian) and harmonic minor in E [S, GW Open strings]; Phrygian and Aeolian are the band's "core harmonic essentials" (said of the riffs, not his solos) [S, GW 10 techniques]. [I] Over Phrygian riffs (a b2 chord move), add the b2 to the pentatonic; harmonic minor (raised 7th) over a major V or at a solo's darkest moment, about one phrase in four.
- Chord-led choice, from Satriani: "what scale was most appropriate for what chord progressions", "pick the notes I wanted... as opposed to just going for a scale that covered it all", "when to go major or minor" [S, GW RTL]; Satriani taught him "to decipher the song's tonality" over thrash's odd progressions [S, GW Satriani recalls]. He outlines a modulation near a solo's end [S, GW Prime Cuts]. [I] Chord tones of the chord under the bar on phrase starts and held notes.
- Chromatic lines: a chromatic lick all the way down the top string; a two-note pull-off moved chromatically up against an open-string pedal, "outside" notes that make sense because the pedal repeats and the line resolves back into key [S, GW Prime Cuts, GW Open strings].
- Diminished: a four-note diminished chord exercise from Satriani played over a break's four accents [S, GW Prime Cuts]; modal playing and "broken arpeggios" (Malmsteen's term) in his longest early solo [S, GW Prime Cuts]. Today he plays down modes, "Hungarian scales, symmetrical scales" and dislikes sweep arpeggios over each chord ("sounds like an exercise") [S, GW Trolls]. [I] Diminished arpeggio at most once a solo, on accents; no sweeps.

## 2. Density and space
- "Fast 16th note repetitive patterns that sound intense" [S, MR Play like]; sextuplet runs "very effective at high tempos" [S, MR]; nine notes in half a bar [S, GW 10 techniques]. "A combination of fast playing and long, sustained notes" [S, GW Load].
- Long solos broken into sections, not "one long spew of notes"; "dynamics and melody" to hold attention [S, GW Prime Cuts]. Slow solos with "a lot of space in it" [S, GW Prime Cuts].
- [I] Melodic sections 4-8 notes a bar; repeated-lick bursts 12-24 (16ths, sextuplets); a held note of 1-2 beats every 2 bars; 8-16 bars in a song, 32+ for a long piece.

## 3. Phrasing
- Shape of a solo he knows well live: the first 7-8 bars fixed, then improvised for 20-24 bars, then the last 4 bars as everyone knows them [S, GW MoP bored, GW Kill 'Em All]; early solos: the first 4-8 bars set, the rest improvised [S, GW Hardwired]. [I] A melodic statement, a development, the opening's idea back at the end.
- Melody first: "something really melodic at the beginning of the solo", a melodic opening leading into the fast part, a middle ground "between the really melodic solo at the beginning and the fiery solo at the end" [S, GW Prime Cuts]. Vocal-like melodies [S, GW Trolls].
- Repetition is the engine: repeating licks are "a staple Hammett phrasing idea" [S, GW 10 techniques]; repetitive three-note-per-string pentatonic and blues licks [S, MR]. [I] A lick repeated 4-8 times, then broken off.
- A pivot note picked fast "machine-gun" style between hammer-ons [S, MR]; an open string as a pedal or drone under a moving line [S, GW Open strings].
- Endings: his habit was to "wind it down and end on a low note"; Ulrich's coaching: "ending it dripping up high" and "play through the bar, don't stop after four" [S, GW Hardwired]. [I] End high on a held, vibratoed note half the time.
- Harmonised lines (thirds, and "minor thirds, major thirds and fifths" mixed) [S, GW Prime Cuts, GW 10 techniques]; doubled solos [S, GW Prime Cuts].

## 4. Rhythm
- Descending pentatonic triplets with every note picked "to give it extra bite" [S, MR]; sextuplet groupings [S, MR]; triplet-phrased repeating three-note figures [S, GW 10 techniques].
- Displacement: a repeating lick three beats long against 4/4, so it shifts across the bar [S, GW 10 techniques].
- Starts off the downbeat: "I come in on the upbeat of the third measure of a bar" (Black Album) [S, GW Prime Cuts]. [I] Pickups of 1-3 eighths before the bar; fast runs on the grid, held notes slightly late (0-20 ms).
- Tremolo picking and fast alternate picking [S, LL Through the Never, LL Holier].

## 5. Bends
- "Expressive bends", "from subtle pitch inflections to aggressive bends" [S, LL Holier]; a bend and release inside the repeating three-beat lick [S, GW 10 techniques]; a semitone bend and release on a double stop [S, GW 10 techniques, a riff example]. Out-of-tune, "kinda shaky" bends on early records, which he regrets [S, GW Kill 'Em All, GW Prime Cuts].
- [I] Whole step 60%, half step 30%, 1.5 steps 10%; bend-and-release common inside repeated licks; 1 bend a bar in melodic sections, none in 16th bursts.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Filled with vibrato" on sustained notes, "consistent, wide" [S, LL Holier]; "expressive vibrato" [S, LL Through the Never].
- [I] Wide (+/-0.5 semitone) at about 6 Hz on notes of a beat or more, from about 30% into the note.

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato: "a significant part of Hammett's soloing vocabulary" [S, LL Through the Never]; hammer-on/pull-off runs climbing the neck over an open-string pedal [S, GW Open strings]; pull-off licks [S, GW 10 techniques, MR].
- Trills between two notes [S, LL Holier]; slides joining positions [S, LL Holier, LL Through the Never].
- Double stops, a lot of them in the early years, then less, then crammed into solos again [S, GW Prime Cuts].
- Pinch harmonics, harmonics with whammy tricks [S, LL Through the Never]; Floyd Rose dives [S, GW Trolls]; right-hand tapping in a solo [S, GW Prime Cuts]; slide (standard tuning) on Load [S, GW Load].
- Open strings in leads: easy to reach anywhere on the neck, "bright and twangy" [S, GW Open strings].
- Wah: he uses it "as much as" anyone, but never writes with it; added after for "more intensity and more emotion", not on every downbeat [S, GW Never wrote with wah, GW Wah]. Out of scope.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Wah (by far his biggest sound), Tube Screamer boost, double-tracking, the neck pickup's warmth, whammy dives and harmonic squeals past the bend range, pick attack, backward guitar, slide's tone.

## 9. Recipe
1. Minor pentatonic in the key; the b5 as a target in repeated licks; the b2 over Phrygian chord moves, harmonic minor's raised 7th over a major V.
2. Enter on an upbeat (the "and" of a beat in the bar's second half); open with 4-8 bars of a vocal-like pentatonic melody, chord tones on held notes, wide vibrato.
3. Develop: a short lick repeated 4-8 times; one version three beats long so it drifts across the bar.
4. Burst: a bar of picked descending triplets or sextuplets, or a pivot note picked fast between hammer-ons.
5. Colour: a chromatic run down one string or a chromatic pull-off pair over a repeated pedal note, resolving into key; a trill; a double-stop phrase.
6. Contrast: alternate fast bars with a long sustained note (1-2 beats) every 2 bars; at most one diminished arpeggio, on accents.
7. Return: the opening idea back in the last 4 bars; outline any modulation.
8. End high on a held bent or vibratoed note ("dripping up high"), playing through the bar line, not stopping on bar 4.

## Notes on the sources
- The MusicRadar and Guitar World "10 techniques" lessons are writers' licks; kept only what they say Hammett does (picking every note, repeated licks, the three-beat lick, pentatonics). The LickLibrary pages are lesson descriptions in a marketing voice (vibrato, legato, trills, harmonics); treat their adjectives as weak evidence.
- Era matters: Satriani heard him use "more exotic scales" as the band went on [GW Satriani recalls], and he used modes and broken arpeggios in 1984 [GW Prime Cuts], but in 2023 he puts pentatonics first and rejects arpeggio-per-chord soloing [GW Trolls]. The generator should default to pentatonic/blues with exotic colour sparingly.
- Phrygian dominant "in many a Kirk Hammett solo" (Premier Guitar's Mediterranean surf primer) was seen only in a search summary; the page fetched had no such text, so it isn't used.
- Phrygian/Aeolian as "core" is said of Metallica's riffs, not of his solos.
- Wikipedia could not be reached.
