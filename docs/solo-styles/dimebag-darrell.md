# Dimebag Darrell: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Notes per bar, bend frequency and vibrato speed are in no article and stay [I]. Many lesson examples are the writers' own "Dimebag-inspired" licks; only habits a source states as his are kept. His own Riffer Madness columns (Guitar World) are the strongest sources: he wrote them himself.

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic / "pentatonic blues scale" the base [S, Dime columns, Dime practice tips, LL Solos]; E blues scale licks [S, GW Shred]; natural minor with harmonic-minor touches, brief diminished and whole-tone moments in one solo [S, LL Cemetery Gates]; Phrygian dominant flavour [S, GW Shred].
- His own words: he knew "the major scale, the minor scale, the pentatonic blues scale and the chromatic scale... that's about it"; "in my style there are no rules" [S, Dime columns].
- The major 3rd over minor, a core habit [S, GW 10 Commandments 1]: the "Dimebag Scale" = minor pentatonic + b5 + major 6th + major 3rd. All five "wrong" notes used as focal points (b9, 3, b5, 6, 7) [S, GW 10 Commandments 2].
- Chromatics [S, Dime Q&A: "One word... chromatics!"]: a lick moved up or down the neck a fret at a time, as far as you like; chromatic passing notes between scale notes [S, GW 10 Commandments 2, GW Shred].
- A "bad note" is fixed by bending, sliding or the bar [S, Dime columns]. [I] Pentatonic notes 65%, blues/added (b5, 3, 6) 20%, chromatic passing 15%; phrase ends on the root, 5th or b7 (bent).

## 2. Density and space
- "Floor it" when the solo comes [S, GW Star guitarists, Holcomb]; shred next to "soulful... memorable and singable" lines [S, GW Star guitarists, Heafy]; a solo that "begins softly with some melodic phrases before escalating into faster runs", slow bends against rapid picking, knowing "when to let a note breathe" [S, LL Cemetery Gates]; sustained notes as contrast [S, LL Solos].
- Solos worked out, not winged (takes comped into one) [S, PG Walk, from search summary only: page not readable here]. In the studio the rhythm guitar drops out under the solo [S, MR 1994].
- [I] Melodic stretches 3-6 notes a bar; runs 16-24 (16ths, sextuplets); 0.5-1 beat of rest between phrases; 8-16 bars.

## 3. Phrasing
- A motif repeated an octave higher or lower, also at a solo's start [S, GW best of columns, editor's note]. [I] State a 1-2 bar idea, repeat it an octave away.
- Symmetrical runs: one fingering shape moved across all strings [S, Dime columns, GW 10 Commandments 1]; wide-stretch licks (EVH influence) [S, Dime columns]; a lick moved chromatically [S, Dime Q&A].
- Repeating cells against the beat: an eight-note pattern in a 16th-triplet feel so each repeat lands elsewhere [S, GW Shred].
- Mostly legato despite strong picking (hammer-ons, pull-offs) [S, GW 10 Commandments 1]; alternate-picked blues-scale bursts with string skips, descending pull-off and sweep figures with a southern/country feel [S, GW Shred].
- Climax and close: bends, vibrato and legato, then harmonics and dive bombs at a song's end [S, LL Cemetery Gates, LL Solos]; harmonic screams to "sing out" like the vocalist's screams [S, Dime harmonics 2].
- [I] Calm opening, faster runs mid-solo, a bend/vibrato peak, a whammy or harmonic flourish last.

## 4. Rhythm
- Syncopated rhythms named in his solos [S, LL Solos]; 16th-triplet/sextuplet groupings [S, GW Shred]; playing behind the groove without rushing "let your ears decide when you should hit the next note" (said of riffs) [S, Dime Q&A].
- [I] Runs in 16ths and sextuplets on the grid (tight; humanize small, +/-5-10 ms); held bends land on beats 1 or 3.

## 5. Bends
- "Texas style": approach a target from a half step below and bend into it, often with a pick squeal; a "slow, deliberate half-step bend" so the ear waits for the pitch [S, Dime columns, GW 10 Commandments 2]. Micro-bends [S, GW 10 Commandments 2].
- Wide bends controlled by ear, reinforced with several fingers [S, Dime Q&A]; double-stop and unison bends [S, LL Solos, LL Cemetery Gates]; bend and release ("greasy") [S, Dime columns]; bends on chords and low strings too [S, Dime columns].
- [I] Half step 40% (Texas style, from below), whole 40%, 1.5-2 steps 20%; 1-2 bends a bar in melodic stretches; half-step scoops 150-300 ms (deliberate), whole steps 80-150 ms.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Aggressive vibrato" [S, LL Solos]; "emotive vibrato" from the blues [S, LL Cemetery Gates]; "bend a note, shake it and listen" [S, Dime rut column]; vibrato on bends and power chords as well as single notes [S, Dime columns].
- Whammy-bar vibrato on harmonics after a scream [S, GW 10 Commandments 1, GW Shred].
- [I] Finger vibrato 6-7 Hz, +/-0.5 semitone on held notes; bar vibrato wider (+/-1 semitone) and faster at a phrase end.

## 7. Other techniques
- **Harmonic screams** (his signature) [S, Dime harmonics 2/3]: dump the Floyd Rose, touch a natural harmonic while the string still moves, pull the bar back up to pitch, or past it ("about two-and-a-half steps" up on the G string; a harmonic raised a minor third over its pitch). [I] MIDI: a high note (+24..+36 over the string) entering 5-12 semitones low, rising to pitch over 0.25-0.5 beat, maybe on +3..+5 semitones, then bar vibrato.
- **Whammy pedal:** he says the screams are bar and harmonics, not a Whammy pedal [S, Dime harmonics 2]; he did use a DigiTech Whammy, two octaves up, stepped on for squeals in a riff (Becoming) [S, MR 1994, GW Gear], in solos too [S, MR 5 songs]. [I] Occasional +12/+24 jumps on a held note.
- **Dive bombs** [S, LL Solos, LL Cemetery Gates]. [I] At a solo's or song's end; -12 or more over 1-2 beats.
- Pinch harmonics in leads [S, LL Solos], tapped harmonics, natural harmonics incl. "in-between" ones [S, Dime harmonics 1, GW 10 Commandments 1]; two-handed tapping, sweep picking [S, LL]; long slides "from nowhere", only the destination matters [S, Dime columns]; a two-hand sliding "fake echo" [S, Dime Q&A]; a pick-edge "chirp" over a released bend [S, Dime Q&A]; wide-stretch trills (2-4 frets) [S, Dime practice tips]; wah in solos [S, MR This Love, PG search summary].

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone (solid-state Randall, scooped mids, cascaded EQ gain, noise gate) [S]; tuning about 60 cents flat [S, GW best of columns]; pick squeal, scrape, chirp and "fake echo" sounds; the harmonic's timbre; wah and flanger; dives deeper than the bend range (a scream's 12-semitone scoop and a dive need +/-12 or more: the profile's BEND RANGE decides, else shrink them).

## 9. Recipe
1. Minor pentatonic / blues scale in a box, with the major 3rd, major 6th and b5 added; chromatic passing notes allowed anywhere.
2. Open melodic: a 1-2 bar idea of a few notes with a Texas-style half-step bend from below and aggressive vibrato; rests.
3. Repeat the idea an octave higher (or lower).
4. Build: a legato run (hammer-ons/pull-offs) or an alternate-picked blues-scale burst in 16ths or sextuplets.
5. Displace: an eight-note cell looped in 16th triplets so it shifts against the beat; or a lick moved chromatically up a fret at a time.
6. Symmetrical run: one shape across the strings, descending, at full speed.
7. Peak: a wide bend (1.5-2 steps) or unison/double-stop bend held with heavy vibrato; a pinch harmonic on a high note.
8. Close: a harmonic scream (scoop up to a high note, bar vibrato) or a dive bomb on the root.

## Notes on the sources
- The best are his own columns (techniques for riffs and leads in his words). The lesson writers (Marano's "Commandments", Griffiths) give names like the "Dimebag Scale" that are theirs, not his; their examples are "Dimebag-inspired", so only their stated habits are kept.
- Whammy pedal: he denies it for harmonic screams (bar only) but used it for riff squeals and later solos; both are true for different sounds.
- Lick Library pages are course blurbs, not analyses; their technique lists are kept only as "used in these solos". One gives the Cowboys From Hell solo as D minor pentatonic, which suggests the transcriber's tuning, not a habit.
- Premier Guitar (Electric Etudes, Secrets of the Masters: Walk) could not be read here; the two claims from them came from search summaries and are marked so. Wikipedia was not reachable.
