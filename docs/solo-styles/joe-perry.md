# Joe Perry: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = a source supports it, **[I]** = inference (the researcher could read only search summaries; the sources describe his style in words, without measurements).

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic + blues scale (the b5) as the base [S]; major pentatonic and Mixolydian mixed in [S] ("Beatles/Stones-style rock'n'roll", "occasionally a Mixolydian flavour").
- [I] About 65% minor pentatonic (b5 as a passing note), 35% major pentatonic/Mixolydian (2, major 3rd, 6). Over major/dominant chords: the b3 slid or bent up to the 3. Phrase ends on the root, b7 or 5.
- [I] Loose chord-tone targeting (mainly the downbeat ending a phrase); strict targeting is Brad Whitford's style, not Perry's.

## 2. Density and space
- "Even playing manic solos, he doesn't sound in a hurry"; "tight yet loose"; "economy of notes" [S].
- [I] 4-10 notes a bar; bursts of 12-16 (swung 16ths) for half a bar to a bar; 1-4 beats' rest between phrases; 8-16 bar solos.
- [I] Arc: a sparse bluesy start with a bend, denser repeated licks, a high climax (repeated bends or tremolo-like repeats), a short resolving tag.

## 3. Phrasing
- "Highly rhythmic" solos; rarely the same twice; what works melodically over practice-pattern licks [S].
- [I] Phrases of 1-2 bars, often call and response; a short motif repeated 2-4 times before it breaks; starts off the beat or with a pickup bend; ends on a held, vibrato'd note or a short double-stop stab; no scale runs.

## 4. Rhythm
- "Trademark swung 16th notes and off-beat rhythms"; loose and slinky; "fast and loose" beside Whitford's precision; embraces mistakes [S].
- [I] 16ths swung about 58-62%; 30-40% of starts off the 8th grid; up to 10-25 ms behind the beat (especially bends); timing jitter +/-10-15 ms, velocity spread +/-15; 8ths, swung 16ths, quarter-note bends, some 8th triplets.

## 5. Bends
- Bluesy bends, double-stop bends (one string bent, another ringing), unison bends [S]; "lazy, behind-the-beat bends" (one source) [S].
- [I] 1-3 bends every 2 bars; mostly whole steps, some half, rarely 1.5; bend-and-release often, pre-bends rare; up over 1/8-1/4 beat; double-stop/unison about 20% of bends, mostly at the climax; targets 4 -> 5 and b7 -> root.

## 6. Vibrato
- Wide, vocal-like, crucial to his soloing [S].
- [I] About +/-0.5-1 semitone at 5-6 Hz, on notes of half a beat or longer, bend tops and phrase ends; delayed about 30% of the note.

## 7. Other techniques
- Pull-offs, hammer-ons, slides ("slippery") [S]; Chuck Berry double stops [S]; slide guitar in open G/A/E and DADGAD [S]; harmonies with Whitford [S].
- [I] A slide into about 1 in 4 phrase starts (from 1-3 semitones below); pull-off pairs and triplets in the pentatonic box; double stops 3rds/4ths on the top two strings; repeated-note licks (3-6 hits of one pitch, or a bend repeated); an occasional chromatic passing tone (about 10%), no long chromatic runs; one or two pentatonic boxes, a box higher for the climax.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone and gear (Les Paul, Dan Armstrong, amps, wah, fuzz, Klon), the talk box, real slide guitar (pitch bend only approximates it), pick attack, muted-string noise, feedback.

## 9. Recipe
1. A key centre: minor pentatonic + b5; add the major 3rd, 6 and 2 over major/dominant chords.
2. 8-16 bars: sparse, repeated licks, a high climax, a tag.
3. 1-2 bar motifs starting off the beat, answered with a variation, repeated 2-4 times.
4. Swung 16ths about 60%, a third of starts syncopated, 1-4 beats' rest between phrases.
5. A slow whole-step bend (often bent and released) on a strong note in most phrases; double-stop or unison bends at the climax.
6. Slides into notes, pull-off groups, repeated-note hits; a box higher for the climax.
7. Phrase ends on long notes with wide vibrato (+/-0.5-1 semitone, about 5.5 Hz).
8. Loose humanizing: late bends, +/-10-15 ms jitter, uneven velocities.

Where sources disagree: most say minor pentatonic and blues only; MusicRadar and one lesson add major pentatonic and Mixolydian. Bends are called "precise" in one lesson and "lazy, behind the beat" in another: read as accurate pitch, relaxed timing [I].
