# Clint Lowery: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. **The evidence is thin:** no lesson or article on these sites breaks down his solos, and he says himself that Sevendust's records went years without big leads [S, GW Truth Killer]. What is sourced is his own account of his approach, his training and his influences, plus one writer's notes on the 2023 album. Scales, notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no source and stay [I]. The band's riffs (detuned, chromatic, the "inverted bend motif" of one song, which is John Connolly's) are not used as evidence for his solos.

## 1. Note choice
- His stated priority: "It's not about how complex your phrases are, or what type of dexterity you have, it's just about the notes you choose, your style and your stamp" [S, GW Truth Killer]. Note choice over speed.
- Dissonance mixed in on purpose: he credits Trent Reznor's playing "in terms of mixing dissonance into his overall style" [S, GW Truth Killer]. [I] One or two deliberately sour notes a phrase (b2, b5, a major 7th against minor), held long enough to be heard, then resolved by a half step.
- Melodic leaning: he names Chris Cornell's "melody choices" and Alice in Chains' harmonies as inspirations for his own writing [S, MR Guitarist], and wants to "go heavy but obviously bring a melodic flavour" [S, MR Guitarist] (said of songwriting, not solos specifically).
- His teacher Robbie Green (taught by Jimmy Herring): "watching Robbie's phrasing and note choices ... helped me understand a lot more about music" [S, MR 5 minutes].
- [I] No source names a scale. Default: natural minor / minor pentatonic in the key (the shred players he names), chord tones of the chord under the bar on phrase starts and held notes; the chromatic and dissonant colour above on top.

## 2. Density and space
- Capable of a lot of speed, held back in the band: "I have a lot of speed to my playing and I have a lot of elements that I don't really show within the Sevendust songs ... I've held that back a little bit" [S, GW 2012].
- Fast playing must be clean and meant: "every note needed to have its own area code and definition" (his teacher's rule, repeated as his own about Vai: "Each note he plays means something") [S, MR 5 minutes, MR Tips]; speed before the fundamentals is "a big, sloppy mess" [S, MR Tips]. "The best fast playing is built on taste and intelligence" [S, MR Tips].
- Restraint in arrangement: the band's records have long stretches with no solos at all [S, GW Truth Killer]; on the 2023 album leads were "tastily mapped-out" [S, GW Truth Killer, the writer].
- [I] Solos short (8 bars, 16 at most); melodic bars 4-8 notes; at most one burst bar of 12-16 notes (16ths, picked) per 4 bars; a held note of 1-2 beats closing each 2-bar phrase.

## 3. Phrasing
- Planned rather than jammed: the 2023 solo's phrasing is "tastily mapped-out", informed by Jake E. Lee, Steve Vai and Randy Rhoads [S, GW Truth Killer, the writer]. Vai is "my number one ... more of an extremist, very vast in what he did stylistically" [S, MR 5 minutes]; Dimebag "probably the biggest influence" [S, GW 2012].
- Variation of what he knows: "if you learn something, try to play the same thing a little bit differently and add your own flavor to it" [S, GW Black Out]. [I] State a 1-2 bar idea, repeat it changed (a different ending, an octave up, one note moved).
- [I] Shape: melodic statement, a repeat with variation, one fast picked or legato run, a held resolving note.

## 4. Rhythm
- Alternate picking for speed, from his teacher: "speed playing, more alternate picking-based stuff" [S, MR 5 minutes]; built his technique "with legato and alternate picking" [S, GW Black Out].
- [I] Fast runs as even picked 16ths on the grid; melodic notes on 8ths with some syncopation; slightly behind the beat (0-15 ms) on held notes. No source on triplets or displacement.

## 5. Bends
- Bends and vibrato named as "the most important aspects to guitar playing" (his advice to players) [S, MR Tips].
- [I] Sizes unsourced: whole step 60%, half step 30%, 1.5 steps 10%; about one bend a melodic bar, none in fast runs; a bend onto a dissonant note then released to the chord tone.

## 6. Vibrato
- "A looser, vibe-based approach to vibrato" on one 2023 solo [S, GW Truth Killer, the writer].
- [I] Medium-wide (+/-0.4 semitone), a bit slow and uneven (4.5-5.5 Hz), on notes of a beat or more, starting about 30% into the note.

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato: core to him; he chooses a solo guitar by it: "If I can do legato comfortably, then I can do a guitar solo on it" [S, GW Truth Killer]; legato and alternate picking his practice [S, GW Black Out]. [I] Runs of 3-note-per-string hammer-on/pull-off groups, every other run legato instead of picked.
- Tapping: "explosive tapping" on one 2023 song, with layers of octaver scale work and tremolo-picked ambience [S, GW Truth Killer, the writer]. [I] At most one tapped figure a solo.
- Whammy pitch-shifting (the Kemper's, its latency "dirty and grimy", which he likes) [S, GW Rig tour]: an effect, out of scope.
- Leads in standard tuning while the band's rhythms are baritone: "with that baritone-ish, heavier gauge, you obviously can't do a guitar solo well" [S, GW Truth Killer]. [I] Place the solo in the upper register of the profile's range, not on the low strings.
- Overdubbed colour: "'verbed-out delays", small overdubs, octaver layers [S, GW Truth Killer]; Phase 90, EVH Flanger, Uni-Vibe, fuzzes [S, GW Rig tour].

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Whammy pedal shifts, octaver layers, delays and reverb, phaser/flanger/Uni-Vibe, the multi-tracked layering, tremolo-picked ambience as texture, pick attack.

## 9. Recipe
1. Key's natural minor / minor pentatonic [I]; chord tones on phrase starts and held notes; one or two dissonant notes a phrase (b2, b5, major 7th), resolved by a half step.
2. Upper register (standard-tuning lead over low rhythm parts); 8-16 bars.
3. Open with a 1-2 bar melodic idea, 4-8 notes a bar, a held note with loose vibrato at its end.
4. Repeat the idea changed: new ending, an octave up, or one note moved.
5. One fast run per 4 bars: picked 16ths, every note distinct (even velocities, no ghosted notes), or a legato 3-note-per-string run.
6. Optionally one tapped figure near the climax.
7. A bend (whole step mostly) onto a tense note, released to the chord tone.
8. End on a held chord tone with loose vibrato; leave space (the solo is short, the song comes first).

## Notes on the sources
- Only the Guitar World *Truth Killer* feature (2023) describes his actual leads, and those lines (phrasing "mapped-out", "looser" vibrato, tapping, octaver work, the Reznor dissonance) are the writer's descriptions, not lessons; the "notes you choose" quote and the legato line are his own.
- His teacher's "area code" rule and his "slow down ... bend notes, learn vibrato" are advice to others; taken as his values, weak evidence for how often he does anything.
- Influences (Vai, Malmsteen, Gilbert, Van Halen, Rhoads, Dimebag, Jake E. Lee, Maiden, Dio's guitarists) are not his habits; nothing here borrows their techniques as his.
- Several interviews (2008, 2012) say he was "getting back into the flashier stuff" and wanted to "shred it up" on a next record: his solo style shown on record is more restrained than his ability.
- No Call Me No One interview about solos was found. Premier Guitar's Rig Rundown page is a video with no text. The Cold Day Memory interview, the PRS review and the Gary Noon interview were read and had nothing on his leads.
