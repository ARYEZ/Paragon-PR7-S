# Zakk Wylde: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Bend sizes, vibrato speed and most notes-per-bar counts are in no article and stay [I]. Lesson examples are mostly the writers' own licks "in the style"; only habits a source states as Wylde's (or he states himself) are kept.

## 1. Note choice
- Pentatonic first, by choice: he crossed off what made others famous (harmonic minor, diminished, sweeps, classical arpeggios, tapping, whammy, three-notes-per-string diatonic runs) and "all that was left was pentatonics" [S, MR 10 tips, GW 15 tips, GW Combination, GP Five tips, MR No More Tears]; "mainly picked pentatonic ideas" [S, GW Combination].
- Minor pentatonic and the blues scale (minor pentatonic + b5) are the core [S, GW 15 tips, GW 5 lessons]; "straight-out pentatonic licks" [S, GW 1989].
- Major pentatonic for the bright, Southern/Allman side: the minor box moved down three frets; "they'd always be shifting from major to minor"; "whether your thirds are minor or major" sets the sound [S, MR No More Tears, MR 10 tips]. [I] Over major-key ballads, major pentatonic; over minor riffs, minor/blues; switch with the chord.
- Passing tones: "a note that would sound wrong if you stopped on it" but touched on the way [S, GW 15 tips]; chromatics and "little slides" from country (Albert Lee) and jazz (Pat Martino) licks learned "from a more bluesy frame of mind" [S, MR 10 tips, MR No More Tears, GP Five tips]. [I] One chromatic passing note per fast run at most, never on a beat's start or a held note.
- Notes chosen by the song: "a solo should be part of the song"; the chord progressions are written to take the solos [S, GW Combination, MR No More Tears]; in other players' solos, keep the "signature notes" [S, GP Other solos]. [I] Phrase ends on the chord's root or 5th.

## 2. Density and space
- Fast, "machine-gun" picked pentatonic runs are his trademark [S, GW Combination, GW 5 lessons]; but Ozzy "didn't want me to play very fast on every cut" [S, GW 1989], and slow, emotional bends matter as much (his teenage turn from speed to "phrasing, vibrato or string bending") [S, GW 15 tips].
- Shape: "a melody thing and then maybe put the afterburners on near the end" (the Rhoads / Neal Schon school); "a beginning, a middle and an end" [S, GW Engines, MR No More Tears]; one solo: pentatonic licks at the start, then a big ascent "fitting along to the chords going up" at the end [S, MR No More Tears].
- [I] Melodic opening 3-8 notes a bar with 1-2 beats' rest; middle bursts of 16ths (16 a bar); the end sextuplets (24 a bar) or 32nds; 8-16 bars in a song.

## 3. Phrasing
- Composed, not jammed: solos pieced together section by section over a backing track; outro jams improvised [S, GW Engines, GW Combination].
- "Bending and phrasing notes, jumping strings – much closer to how a singer sings" [S, GW 1989]; "think of your vibrato like you're singing" [S, GP Five tips].
- Short picking cells repeated and moved: a cell on the top two strings carried up through all five pentatonic boxes, "the faster you play it, the cooler it sounds" [S, GW Brewtality 3]; the same cell given new rhythms [S]; familiar licks played backwards or recombined "like the combination on a lock" [S, GW Combination].
- Whole-neck movement: boxes "connected" by slides (up with the ring finger, down with the index) so a run covers a wide stretch of the neck [S, GW Brewtality linking, GW 15 tips]; boxes stacked into wide-stretch shapes [S, GW 15 tips].
- Phrase endings carry the vibrato: weak vibrato on "that final note" ruins it [S, GP Five tips]. [I] 1-2 bar phrases, every one ending on a held note with wide vibrato or a pinch harmonic.

## 4. Rhythm
- Two-notes-per-string pentatonic cells in 16ths and sextuplets; six-note groups descending three strings at a time, played as 16ths so the group's start drifts off the beat (every 1.5 beats), or as sextuplets on the beat [S, GW Journey, GW 5 lessons]; a six-note group in 32nds for the climax [S, GW Journey: the writer's line, but "trademark" patterns].
- "Afterburner" (his word): the next scale note above a two-string cell added with the pinky [S, GW Journey].
- Three-note figures on the top two strings swept down and picked up [S, GW 5 lessons, MR No More Tears]. Triplets that speed up to 16ths [S, GW 5 lessons].
- [I] On the grid (picked, aggressive), timing jitter under 10 ms; accents on each cell's first note.

## 5. Bends
- "Try playing around with different speeds of string bending... you don't want to sound like you've had 650 cups of coffee – not all the time"; "explore all the little shades of the bend" [S, GP Five tips].
- Bend and tap now and then: the G string bent round the 7th fret and the top note tapped [S, GW 1989, MR How to play]; a pre-bend a tone with notes ringing over it [S, MR How to play].
- [I] Whole step 60%, half step 20%, 1.5 steps 15%, pre-bend and release 5%; 1-2 bends a bar in the melodic part, one at each phrase end in the fast part; up in 60-150 ms, varied (his point); a held bend gets the wide vibrato.

## 6. Vibrato
- The signature: wide, "no vibrato too wide in the Wylde world", from the forearm and wrist, at least a tone and "a minor 3rd... more Zakk-like" (on pinch harmonics) [S, GW 5 lessons]; "awesome wide vibrato" [S, MR How to play]; "muscular" [S, GW 15 tips].
- "More of a smashing into the song than a slowly creeping-in approach": full width from the start; narrower on ballads but still his [S, MR 10 tips].
- [I] Width +/-1 semitone on plain notes, 2-3 semitones (up from the note, as a bend) on pinch harmonics and phrase ends; 5-7 Hz; starts within 30 ms of the note.

## 7. Other techniques
- Pinch harmonics: "trademark pinched harmonic vibrato" [S, GW 5 lessons]; squeals "in just about any gap that opens up" [S, GW Squealers]; mixed into palm-muted riffs for contrast [S, MR How to play]; heavily vibrato'd on the low strings round the 3rd, 5th or 6th fret, made common by him [S, GW Journey]. [I] One pinch at most every two bars in a solo, mostly at phrase ends, sounding 1-2 octaves (+ a 5th) over the fretted note.
- Picking: strict alternate picking as the base, economy "strike through" on fast descending top-two-string licks [S, MR 10 tips, GW 15 tips, GW Brewtality 3].
- Chicken picking, hybrid picking, double stops, string skipping (from Albert Lee) [S, GW 15 tips, GW 1989, MR How to play]; slides, hammer-ons and pull-offs ("slurs") [S, GW 15 tips]; slide guitar in Southern songs [S, MR No More Tears, GW 1989]; touch harmonics [S, MR How to play].
- Wah: he wanted more on the first album, Ozzy said "Hendrix" [S, GW 1989, GW Ozzy rules]; a Dunlop wah still on his board [S, GP Technique]. No whammy bar, so no dive bombs [S, GW 1989, GP Technique].

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone (Les Paul, JCM800, SD-1 / Berzerker overdrive), the chorus that makes the squeals jump out, doubled tracks [S]; the wah's sweep (a CC at best); pick attack and string noise; the exact harmonic a pinch gives (a sampler articulation: Shreddage's harmonics key or the 127 pinch layer); vibrato wider than the bend range.

## 9. Recipe
1. Pool: minor pentatonic + b5 over minor/riff sections, major pentatonic over major ones; a rare chromatic passing note inside fast runs.
2. Open with a melodic statement: 3-8 notes a bar, bends of varied speed, every phrase end held with wide vibrato that starts at full width.
3. Answer with the same idea moved or varied, maybe a bend-and-tap or a double stop.
4. Middle: a two-string picking cell repeated and carried up through the boxes with slides, 16ths.
5. Push: six-note descending groups as 16ths drifting against the beat, then as sextuplets on it; add the "afterburner" note above.
6. Squeal: a pinch harmonic with a 2-3 semitone vibrato in a gap (at most every two bars).
7. Climax ("afterburners near the end"): a fast ascent following the chords upward, sextuplets or 32nds.
8. End on a high root or 5th: a held bend or pinch harmonic shaken wide.

## Notes on the sources
His "list" changes between tellings: some versions cross off harmonics (GW Combination, MR No More Tears: "taps, harmonics, divebombs") though pinch harmonics are his trademark (read: natural/tapped harmonics, Van Halen's), and "no taps" though he does an occasional bend-and-tap (GW 1989). GW Combination's writer calls his runs "three-note-per-string pentatonic", against his own "two notes per string" (MR 10 tips) and his crossing off three-notes-per-string (GP Five tips); yet GW 15 tips has him practising three-notes-per-string natural minor. The 32nd-note climax, the minor-3rd vibrato width and the sweep-picked three-note figure come from lesson writers (Marano, Griffiths) describing his habits, not from him. No source gives bend sizes, vibrato speed or notes per bar; the Lick Library course page (not listed: marketing text only) and a tab-only Guitar World licks page were read but give nothing usable.
