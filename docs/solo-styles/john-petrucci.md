# John Petrucci: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Bend sizes, vibrato speed and notes per bar in melodic passages are in no article and stay [I]. Several lessons are the writers' own licks "in the style"; only habits a source states as Petrucci's (his own columns and interviews first) are kept.

## 1. Note choice
- Roots: E minor pentatonic, "single-note bluesy rock" (Zeppelin, AC/DC, early Rush) [S, GW Method]; he still uses Lifeson licks learned in high school [S, GW Rush]; bluesy pentatonic passages kept for contrast inside complex songs [S, MR Noodling, TG 2013].
- Diatonic modes as his everyday material: Aeolian/natural minor in every position, three-octave shapes [S, GW Method, GW Positional]; Mixolydian runs [S, GW Betcha]; leads that "switch through major scale modes, harmonic minor, diminished and chromatic ideas" (the interviewer's words, which he accepts: "I weave through different scales and we adjust the chords to work") [S, MR Noodling]; the half-whole diminished scale in his music [S, GW 5 Skills].
- Arpeggios of the key's chords: one note per string through the diatonic sevenths (in E minor: D7, Gmaj7, Cmaj7, F#m7b5), two-per-string arpeggios outlining a progression [S, GW Method, GW Lick]. Triads stacked to imply richer chords (major + relative minor = 6th / m7; I + iii = maj13) over a progression or a one-chord vamp [S, GW Triads].
- Chromatic notes "to play over the bar": non-diatonic but "still going somewhere" [S, TG 2013]; a chromatic passing tone added to make four notes a string, the higher of two choices [S, GW Method]; the chromatic scale "used judiciously... can fit over any chord" [S, GW Chromatic].
- Climax "really outside", e.g. a lick climbing chromatically, to create tension [S, MR Noodling].
- Wide intervals, tones spread apart instead of root-3-5, an open-string pedal rubbing against half steps [S, MR Noodling].
- Melody first: "instead of making a list, I pull out the melody I want to hear most" [S, MR Noodling].
- [I] Chord tones on beat 1 of each chord and at phrase ends; scale per chord (Aeolian/Dorian over minor, Mixolydian over dominant, harmonic minor over a V in minor); 1-2 chromatic passing notes a bar in fast lines, never landing on a strong beat.

## 2. Density and space
- Two modes, deliberately contrasted: "something more bluesy and soulful" against shred, "you need to know how to switch gears" [S, MR Noodling]; a beautiful melodic section put against riff-heavy energy ("slow down and just admire the water") [S, GW 2021, MR 2021].
- Melodic playing as singing: "you can only fit so many syllables in"; Gilmour, Schon, Journey, Floyd, Marillion as the models [S, MR Noodling, MR Top 5, MR 2021].
- Fast passages are even 16ths, sextuplets ("groupings of six") and 16th-note triplets [S, GW Method, GW Positional, GW 5 Skills]; a demonstrated lick runs 16ths at 140 BPM [S, GW Lick].
- [I] Melodic sections 3-6 notes a bar with held notes of 1-2 beats; bursts 16-24 notes a bar (16ths to sextuplets); rests of 1-2 beats between melodic phrases, almost none inside a burst; 8-32 bars.

## 3. Phrasing
- The solo is composition: "part of the composition... like watching a movie... it has to push the story forward" [S, MR Noodling]; "it should contribute to the song" [S, TG 2013].
- Shape: "almost like big curves", building gradually toward the chorus; a crescendo, then something new rather than "more notes"; "there should always be some sort of conclusion or climax" [S, TG 2013, MR Noodling].
- Ideas from the song's melody [S, MR Noodling]; recurring themes and melodies come back near the end of long pieces [S, GW 2021].
- Avoid repeating himself: "Have I heard that before?" then try it another way [S, MR Noodling]. [I] A generator should vary a motif rather than loop it.
- Trading solos with keyboards, each answer taking the last one "somewhere else" [S, TG 2013, GW 2021]; one blues solo "starts out kind of bluesy, then it's kind of jazzy, and it builds and builds" [S, GW 10 Performances].
- Harmony leads: not only 3rds but 3rds, 4ths and 5ths mixed, octaves displaced, or a harmony holding a common tone [S, MR Noodling].
- Sequenced cells: six-note diatonic cells, each started two (or three) scale steps from the last ("up six, back two") while moving up or down the neck [S, GW Positional].

## 4. Rhythm
- The same pattern heard and played both as 8th-note triplets and as straight 16ths [S, GW Method]; six-note cells in 16ths so the shape crosses the beat ("a more unusual melodic shape") [S, GW Positional].
- Odd groupings: a triplet-accented figure made five notes so string changes fall on downstrokes (5/8) [S, GW 5/8]; a descending/ascending sequence falling into 5/4 [S, GW Method]; seven felt as 3+4, 4+3 or 5+2 [S, MR 2021]; phrases that "don't always end on the one" (the interviewer's words, his "Absolutely") [S, MR 2021].
- Staccato bursts are "all about groove and syncopation", accents placed against the downbeat [S, MR Noodling]; locked to the kick and snare [S, MR Top 5].
- Sweeps in deliberate time, even 8th-note sweeps [S, MR Noodling]. Half-time sections for melodic solos [S, MR 2021].
- [I] Timing tight: +/-5 ms on bursts; one odd grouping (5 or 7 in a 16th stream) every 2-4 bars of a fast passage.

## 5. Bends
- Ghost bends (pre-bend and release) learned early [S, GW Method]; "slow bends" and "hanging on notes" in melodic passages, bent exactly in tune ("that's when you are exposed") [S, MR Noodling, MR Top 5].
- [I] Whole step 60%, half step 30%, 1.5 steps 10%; 1 bend a bar in melodic passages, none inside bursts; bends up in 100-200 ms (slow), pre-bend releases 150-250 ms.
- Whammy: slides into notes (Holdsworth's way), the horse whinny, his own tricks, used with care ("one of those ways you can make the guitar sound bad") [S, GW Whammy]; "whammy squiggles and squeals", "heavens-ascending whammy work" at a solo's end (the writers' descriptions) [S, GW 10 Performances]. [I] Dive or rise of 2-12 semitones by the profile's BEND RANGE, at most once or twice a solo.

## 6. Vibrato
- He learned it as "this huge, repeated bend": bend up from one note to another, back down and up [S, GW Method]; "proper vibrato" counted as part of controlling the guitar [S, MR Top 5]; Steve Morse singles out his vibrato [S, GP Morse].
- [I] On notes held a beat or more: about 5-6 Hz, +/-0.5 semitone, starting 100-150 ms after the note; none on notes inside bursts.

## 7. Other techniques
- Alternate picking is the core: "the school of power picking", a period of picking every note after Morse, Di Meola, McLaughlin [S, MR 7 Steps, GP Morse, MR Top 5]; strict down-up even through syncopation, patterns shaped so a string change starts on a downstroke [S, GW 5/8]; picked runs for "a really fast, linear type of run" [S, MR Complacent].
- Mixing techniques: legato, hybrid, sweeps, alternate picking in one solo [S, MR Complacent, MR Top 5, TG 2013]; a sweep up with legato and taps on top at a climax ("video game licks") [S, TG 2013]; tapping used less now [S, GW Practice 2].
- Sweeps: one note per string, each note muted after it sounds so it never rings as a chord [S, GW Sweep, MR Noodling]; chromatically descending m7 arpeggios over altered dominants [S, GW Sweep]; melody notes respread one a string and swept [S, MR Noodling].
- Legato "a la Allan Holdsworth" and trills in his practice; string-skipping "intervallic studies" [S, GW Practice 2]; double pull-offs in descending triplet runs [S, GW Betcha].
- Position shifts: fingers moving up the neck while the line descends (and vice versa); the same shapes in three octaves [S, GW Positional, GW Method]. Unison lines with keyboards [S, GW 5 Skills].

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone (Boogie lead channel, more mids), stereo delay (dotted 8th or 300/600 ms) [S, MR Top 5], wah, harmonics, pick attack and string muting; whammy moves beyond a pitch bend's range; the sweep's left-hand muting (MIDI notes are already separate).

## 9. Recipe
1. Melodic opening over the song's chords: a motif from its melody, 3-6 notes a bar, slow in-tune bends, wide vibrato on held notes.
2. Answer and develop it, with wider intervals (6ths, octaves, an open-string pedal) and triads stacked for colour.
3. Switch gears: a bar of 16th or sextuplet alternate-picked scale cells (six notes, each two steps on), climbing the neck.
4. Displace: a five- or seven-note group or six-note cells in 16ths across the beat, phrases ending off the one.
5. Arpeggios: the key's seventh chords one note a string (swept or picked) through the changes.
6. Breathe: a short bluesy or singing phrase with a bend.
7. Climax: something outside (a chromatic climb, a sweep with legato/taps, or a whammy rise).
8. Close: a held chord tone with vibrato, or straight back into the song's theme.

## Notes on the sources
Premier Guitar's pages would not load here (empty shells), so its lessons were not read. The "Rock Discipline" book/DVD was not available. GW's "5 shred skills" and string-skipping lessons are Charlie Griffiths' and Jenna Scaramanga's own examples in the style; only their statements about his songs (six-note groupings, the diminished scale) are kept, not their septuplet lick. Lick Library's course lists pinched and tapped harmonics and string skipping as his, but says it is not made with him: not used. The modes / harmonic minor / diminished list and the "phrases off the one" idea are the interviewers' words, which he agreed with. The whammy descriptions in "10 Performances" are the writers' hearing of the records. No source gives bend sizes or vibrato speed.
