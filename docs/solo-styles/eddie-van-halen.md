# Eddie Van Halen: lead style (technique sheet, revised from the full articles)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo or melody of his is written out here.
**[S: X]** = the article X supports it, **[I]** = inference (no article gives the number). licklibrary.com refused the download (HTTP 451): its claims stay unconfirmed.

Sources by label: **GP-Buono** Guitar Player, The Essential Eddie Van Halen Guitar Lesson; **GP-Tap** Guitar Player, How to Tap Like EVH; **GP-Scale** Guitar Player, The EVH Scale; **GP-1980** the 1980 Guitar Player interview; **GW-Tap** Guitar World, How to two-hand tap like EVH; **GW-5** Guitar World, 5 ways to make tapping work; **GW-Whammy** Guitar World, 5 ways to improve whammy technique; **GW-Tricks** Guitar World, Sick Guitar Tricks; **MR-6** MusicRadar, 6 tricks from EVH; **PG** Premier Guitar, Electric Etudes: EVH Techniques.

## What changed from the first sheet
- Rhythm: swung, "push-pull" (uptempo shuffles about 230-236 BPM), not straight [PG, GP-Buono]; groupings of 3, 5 and 7 central [GP-Buono]; tapping often in 32nds ("all tapping is triplets" is a misconception) [MR-6].
- Tapped harmonics 12, 7, 5 and 4 frets above the fretted note, not only 12 [GP-Tap].
- Tapped arpeggios sourced: the tap usually at the 12th fret, moved one fret at chord changes (shared notes), each triplet twice in place then down, pedaling patterns, minor-triad sextuplets with the open string.
- New: alternating legato, single-string diminished arpeggios, the inverse Van Halen scale, the open string as a connector, a cell across descending string pairs, an up-a-2nd/down-a-3rd cell at phrase ends, b5/5 back and forth.
- Whammy sourced: pre-dip and release into a note, quick "doops", a slow dive on the low E, the "horse whinny", a high pinch harmonic before the dive (trademark).
- Pick slides: in no article; demoted to an occasional extra [I]. Vibrato numbers: still [I]; only "wide" is sourced.
- What mattered most to him: "Feeling"; some solos worked out, others spontaneous, varied live [GP-1980].

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic + b5 (blues), with b5/5 back and forth (chromatic zigzags) [GP-Buono]. [I] Blues/pentatonic about 60%, chromatic 15%, major or Dorian colour 25%.
- Aeolian and Dorian in tapping and tremolo examples [GW-Tap, PG].
- The Van Halen scale [GP-Scale, GP-Buono]: degrees 1 2 b3 3 4 #4 5 6 b7 7, one half/whole-step fret shape on every string, fast passages only, often six-note fragments palm-muted; fits major/dominant chords, over minor shaded and resolved to the root. Its inverse: whole/half step [GP-Buono].
- Single-string diminished triads (two minor thirds over six frets) [GP-Buono].
- Tapped arpeggios: major/minor triads [GW-Tap], the tap usually at the 12th fret [GW-Tap, GP-Tap], extended with the b7 and 11 [GW-5], the tap moved one fret between chords (shared notes) [PG], crossing strings implies a progression [GW-Tap]. Pedaling: the fretted note fixed while taps move, or the tap fixed while fretted notes move [GP-Tap].

## 2. Density and space
- Bursts: tapping 6 a beat or 8 (32nds); quintuplets and septuplets; 8th triplets at about 236 BPM [GW-Tap, MR-6, GP-Buono, PG]. [I] Melody 1-2 notes a beat.
- Feeling over speed; melodic or spontaneous both fine [GP-1980]. [I] Arc: melodic opening, a burst, melody, a climax ending on a dive or a long vibrato note.
- [I] Rests 20-30% of beats in melody, under 10% in bursts; the open string fills position changes [MR-6].

## 3. Phrasing
- A short cell across descending pairs of strings; five-note cells as quintuplets; tapped triplets twice in place, then down [GP-Buono, GP-Tap].
- Phrase ends: an up-a-major-2nd / down-a-major-3rd cell, or a pair of bends [GP-Buono, PG]. Starts: a note scooped up by releasing a pre-dipped bar [MR-6].
- [I] 1-2 bar phrases in question-and-answer pairs.

## 4. Rhythm
- Swung 8ths, "push-pull"; groupings change within a solo (triplets, 16ths, 5s) [PG, GP-Buono].
- Tremolo picking as a free flurry, the picking hand not anchored; tremolo also walks up a scale [MR-6, PG]. [I] 32nds or faster for 1-2 beats.
- Tapping: triplets (tap-pull-hammer), 16ths with a double pull-off, sextuplets with a double hammer-on, 32nds; a held tap with vibrato dropped into a stream as a break [GW-Tap, MR-6].

## 5. Bends
- Large bends up to a major third; smaller bends with a cut-off note after; a pair of bends to end a phrase [MR-6, PG].
- With tapping: bend and tap (the tap sounds a step higher); a tapped bend released while the tap is held; harmonics tapped on a held bend [GP-Tap, GW-Tap, PG].
- [I] 15-25% of long notes; whole 60%, half 20%, larger 20%.

## 6. Vibrato
- Wide, often on a final held note; on held taps and tapped harmonics too [MR-6, GP-Tap]; bar vibrato after a whinny [GW-Tricks].
- [I] +/-0.5-1 semitone, about 5-7 Hz, from about 30% into the note.

## 7. Other techniques
- Tapping with a straight index finger, the pick palmed [GP-Tap, MR-6]; tapped slides upward with no fixed destination [MR-6, GP-Tap].
- Legato: "alternating legato" (a downstroke, then a double hammer-on, per string), wide stretches, palm-muted ghost hammers [GP-Buono, PG].
- Harmonics: natural at the 5th, 4th and 3rd frets inside open-string arpeggios [GP-Buono, PG]; tapped 12, 7, 5 and 4 frets above [GP-Tap]; a high pinch harmonic before the dive [MR-6].
- Whammy: a pre-dipped bar released into the note, quick doops (about a semitone), a slow dive on the low E, the whinny [MR-6, GW-Whammy, GW-Tricks]. [I] Dives 12-24 semitones over 1-2 beats, about once per section end; flutter +/-1-2 semitones at 10-15 Hz is from a non-EVH example (an extra, not his habit).
- [I] Frets 5-17, taps to the 17th-22nd.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone (cleaner than people think, mids not scooped [PG]), the "brown sound", amp, reverb, echo; harmonic timbres, string noise, behind-the-nut rakes [GW-Tricks]; the real behaviour of the bar.

## 9. Recipe
1. Blues scale on the key with b5/5 chromatic moves; the Van Halen scale (or its inverse) for fast runs, resolved to the root.
2. Swing; groupings change phrase by phrase: triplets, 16ths, a 5 or 7 burst.
3. Open with a bar-scooped note. Melody 1-2 notes a beat; phrases ending on a major-third bend or a pair of bends; wide vibrato on held notes.
4. Bursts, one of: a tapped triad (tap at the 12th) twice in place then down, the tap a fret over at each chord change; a pedaling pattern; alternating legato; 1-2 beats of tremolo.
5. A short cell across descending string pairs; the open string between positions; phrases closed with the up-a-2nd/down-a-3rd cell.
6. Accents: tapped harmonics (12, 7, 5 or 4 frets above) on a held note, a tapped slide, a doop.
7. End: a high pinch harmonic, then a slow dive (or a whinny), or a long wide-vibrato root.
