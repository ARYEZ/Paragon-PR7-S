# Eddie Van Halen: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = a source supports it, **[I]** = inference (the researcher could read only search summaries; guitarworld.com and premierguitar.com weren't reachable from the session).

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic and blues with chromatic passing tones; self-taught from Clapton-era blues licks [S].
- Major and minor pentatonic swapped over one chord [S, a fan-forum analysis: weaker]. Dorian an occasional colour (lessons disagree on single songs) [S].
- [I] Weights: minor pentatonic/blues about 60%, major-pentatonic swaps 15%, the Dorian 6th 10%, chromatic passing 15% (off the beat, into a target on the beat).
- The "Van Halen scale" [S]: one three-notes-per-string fret shape repeated on every string (alternating half and whole steps; degrees 1 2 b3 3 4 #4 5 6 b7 7): fast ascending runs over major or dominant chords.
- Tapped arpeggios [S]: triads and inversions on one string (tap, pull-off to a fretted note, hammer-on, or pull-off to the open string); one lesson a minor triad with b7 and 11; they follow the chords by moving one note a semitone to the next triad (one analysis: modal interchange, harmonic minor). [I] The tap 5-12 frets above, on the root, 3rd or 5th.

## 2. Density and space
- Quintuplets appear [S]. Tapping, dives and harmonics mixed "with a sense of melody"; fast-as-possible "doesn't hold much water"; "not everything needs a guitar solo" [S].
- [I] Bursts 6 notes a beat (sometimes 8); melodic stretches 1-2 a beat with long notes. Rests 20-30% of beats in melodic sections, under 10% in bursts.
- [I] A 16-bar arc: 1-4 melodic with bends, 5-8 a legato or tapped burst, 9-12 melody, 13-16 the climax burst ending on a dive or a held note.

## 3. Phrasing
- [I] Phrases 1-2 bars, question and answer; a 3-6 note cell looped 2-4 times (especially tapping), then broken off; openings a bend, a pick slide or a harmonic; endings a wide-vibrato held note, a dive or a cut-off.

## 4. Rhythm
- Tapping in triplets (tap-pull-hammer), sextuplets for faster runs [S]; tremolo "hummingbird" picking on one note or a short line [S].
- [I] Tremolo in 32nds (or as fast as the tempo allows) for 1-2 beats; straight feel, slightly ahead; a blues swing in slow melodic phrases.

## 5. Bends
- Up to a major third (four semitones), then smaller bends cut short [S].
- [I] 15-25% of long notes in melodic sections; whole step 60%, half 20%, minor/major third 20%; occasional unison bends; bend-and-release; quick (pitch within about 1/16 beat).

## 6. Vibrato
- Wide, aggressive, vocal; tapping without vibrato "sounds like a typewriter" [S].
- [I] +/-0.5-1 semitone at 5-7 Hz, from about 30% into the note; also with the bar.

## 7. Other techniques
- Two-hand tapping with the index (sometimes middle) finger, the pick held away; slides and bends with the tapping finger [S].
- Flowing legato a defining mark [S]; [I] one picked note per 3-4.
- Harmonics: natural; tapped (a node about 12 frets above the fretted note, with vibrato), from Fair Warning on; pinch as accents [S].
- Whammy: dive bombs, small dips, warbles/flutters [S]. [I] Dives once per solo or section end, 12-24 semitones over 1-2 beats; dips -1 to -2 semitones before a note; flutters +/-1-2 semitones at 10-15 Hz for half a beat to a beat.
- Pick slides as an entry [S]; [I] a fast falling glissando or a noise accent in MIDI.
- [I] Register mostly frets 5-17, tapping to 17-22, dives from open harmonics.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
The "brown sound" (hot amps, pickups, phaser, delay, reverb), string noise and pick scrapes, exact harmonic timbres, feedback, the real behaviour of the bar, pick slides (approximated only).

## 9. Recipe
1. Minor pentatonic/blues on the key; major-pentatonic swaps over major chords; chromatic passing notes off the beat.
2. Arc: melodic, burst, melodic, climax burst; 1-2 bar phrases in call-and-response pairs.
3. Melodic phrases: 1-2 notes a beat, a whole-step or larger bend on the longest note, wide vibrato (about +/-0.7 semitone, 6 Hz) on held notes.
4. Bursts, one of: a tapped triplet/sextuplet loop on one string (the chord's triad or an inversion, the tap 5-12 frets above), one note moving a semitone at each chord change; a "Van Halen scale" run; 1-2 beats of tremolo picking.
5. Each burst cell looped 2-4 times, broken off with a bend or a held note.
6. Accents: a pinch or tapped harmonic about every 4 bars; small bar dips before notes.
7. End on a dive (12-24 semitones over 1-2 beats) or a long wide-vibrato root.
