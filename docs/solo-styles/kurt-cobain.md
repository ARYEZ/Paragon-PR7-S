# Kurt Cobain: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. The evidence is thin: no lesson site reachable here breaks his solos down note by note in prose, and he gave almost no interviews about lead playing. Notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no article and stay [I]. Only habits a source states as his are kept.

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic is the base: the In Bloom solo "employs the minor pentatonic scale, particularly E minor pentatonic" [S, LL In Bloom]; "the minor pentatonic scale for melodic and expressive solos" [S, LL Nirvana course].
- Not strictly in the scale: "Cobain did not stick strictly to scales, and he often used chromatic passing tones" [S, LL In Bloom]; "the note choice might be unconventional" [S, MR 5 songs]. [I] One chromatic passing note (a half step between two scale notes, on a weak sixteenth) every 1-2 bars.
- The vocal melody as the source: Come As You Are's solo "simply quoted the vocal melody in a snotty, vibey way" [S, GP 40 solos]; Nevermind's solos include "melodic lines that echo the vocal melody with George Harrison-like restraint" [S, MR Story of Nevermind]; his "primary gift was writing spectacular vocal melodies" [S, GW Grunge guide]; Vig: he "really admired McCartney's melodic sensibility" [S, MR Vig]. [I] The generator takes the song's melody (the MELODY lane or the chord track's melody idea) and replays it an octave down or at pitch, slightly varied.
- The main riff as the source: the In Bloom solo is "based around the song's main riff", "a variation that is melodically rich and harmonically simple" [S, LL In Bloom]; School's solo starts with "a psychedelic extension to the main riff" [S, MR 5 songs]. A melodic solo "over the verse and spreading out over the chorus chord progression" [S, MR 5 songs, About a Girl].
- Resolution: tension built, then "resolving back to the root note" [S, LL In Bloom]. [I] The last note of each 4-bar phrase on the chord's root; chord tones on beat 1.
- Atonal noise as material: solos "from random noises, atonal feedback" [S, MR 5 songs]; "Sonic Youth-style string mangling" (Breed) [S, MR Story of Nevermind]. [I] In the noisy mode, pitches drawn from the whole chromatic range near the top of the neck, clusters of 2-3 adjacent semitones.

## 2. Density and space
- No speed: "Cobain was never one for shredding or virtuosity. He opted for solos that served the song", "without relying on speed or technical showmanship" [S, LL In Bloom]; "minimalistic", "strip down guitar parts to their bare essentials" [S, LL In Bloom].
- Short and rare: "he took about as many solos as Johnny Ramone" [S, GP 40 solos]; yet Nevermind's first four songs all have one [S, MR Story of Nevermind]. Fast runs exist but are the exception (the Shocking Blue cover) [S, MR 5 songs].
- Little thought when cutting loose: "less than you could possibly imagine" goes through his mind [S, MR Story of Nevermind, his words].
- [I] Melodic mode: 2-6 notes a bar, mostly quarters and eighths; one bar in four close to empty (a held note). Noisy mode: bursts of 8-16 notes in a beat or two, then a held noise. 8 bars by default (the length of a verse or chorus), never over 16.

## 3. Phrasing
- Two kinds of solo [S]: melodic ones that state the vocal line or the riff (Come As You Are, In Bloom, Smells Like Teen Spirit, Heart-Shaped Box: "well-constructed melodic solos") and chaotic ones (Breed, School) [S, MR 5 songs, MR Story of Nevermind].
- Mixed inside one solo: School goes from the riff's extension to "big bends, and the type of noisy Cobain lead runs that are almost sarcastic in their delivery", "choosing to massacre it rather than take it seriously" [S, MR 5 songs]; "ironic solos – they weren't real guitar solos" [S, MR Corgan].
- Composed, not only improvised: comparing the 1990 demos with Nevermind shows "his chaos was indeed composed at times" [S, MR 5 songs]. [I] A solo is the same when the section repeats (a fixed seed per solo), not re-rolled every pass.
- Follows the song's form: "the changes loosely follow the rhythm of Cobain's vocal shifts" (said of a song's chords) [S, MR 5 songs]. [I] The solo's phrases start and end where the vocal's phrases did.
- [I] Shape: melody statement (bars 1-4), the same with small changes or an octave up (5-8); the noisy mode adds a breakdown into bends and noise in the second half.

## 4. Rhythm
- His strength is rhythm: "a phenomenal rhythm player" [S, MR 5 songs]; "pocket and the energy" [S, GW Nuno]; "rarely lost the groove" [S, GW Grunge guide]. [I] Melodic solos lock to the grid like the vocal (timing jitter under 10 ms); noise bursts free (+/-30 ms).
- No source describes his lead rhythm further. [I] Mostly eighths following the vocal rhythm; sixteenth bursts only in the noisy mode; no triplet sequences or displacement.

## 5. Bends
- "Double stops and bends" in In Bloom's solo [S, LL In Bloom]; "big bends" in School's [S, MR 5 songs].
- [I] Melodic mode: whole-step bends on a held note at the end of a phrase, about one every 2 bars; noisy mode: big bends (1.5-2 steps) on two strings at once, released slowly, out of tune on purpose (+/-0.3 semitone off the target). He was "so proud" of "roughly played, out-of-tune guitars" [S, GW Final interview], which supports leaving bends a little off.

## 6. Vibrato
- Only "a subtle vibrato on the final note" of a riff is described (School) [S, MR 5 songs].
- [I] Narrow (+/-0.2 semitone) at about 5 Hz on the last note of a phrase only; none on most notes.

## 7. Other techniques
- Double stops [S, LL In Bloom, LL Nirvana course]: [I] two notes a fourth or fifth apart moved together, or a melody note with an open string, in about a third of the bars.
- Slides [S, LL Nirvana course]: [I] into the first note of a phrase from 2-3 semitones below.
- Open strings ringing in riffs [S, LL Nirvana course]. [I] An open low string under a melody note now and then.
- No whammy: "Ever get the urge to use the twang bar? No... Those things are totally worthless" [S, GW Final interview]; his tech disabled the Mustangs' tremolo [S, MR Mustangs]. So **no dive bombs for this profile**, whatever the bend range.
- Fast runs at times (the Shocking Blue cover) [S, MR 5 songs]: [I] at most one pentatonic run a solo, under a bar.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Feedback and "atonal feedback" (a large part of his solos), string mangling and noise, the "brittle ratchety" ring modulator/tremolo/overdrive sound on Heart-Shaped Box's original solo (Albini's Pedal X; Novoselic called it sabotage, Cobain "I think it sounds cool") [S, MR Albini, GP Novoselic], the Small Clone chorus with the DS-1 on Teen Spirit's solo [S, GW Teen Spirit tone], the DS-1 on The Man Who Sold the World's solo [S, GW Effects solos], the SansAmp [S, MR Albini], smashed guitars, detuned strings. [I] A generator can only hint at noise: chromatic clusters, very high notes, out-of-tune bends and pitch-bend wobble; the real thing needs audio.

## 9. Recipe
1. Pick the mode: melodic (default, about 3 solos in 4) or noisy [I]; a fixed seed so a repeated section plays the same solo.
2. Melodic: take the song's vocal melody (or the riff) over the same chords, at pitch or an octave down, in minor pentatonic with one chromatic passing note every 1-2 bars.
3. Keep it sparse: 2-6 notes a bar, eighths and quarters, on the grid; one near-empty bar in four.
4. Vary the second half: the same line with small changes (a note moved a scale step, a rhythm shifted an eighth) or an octave higher; add double stops (fourths/fifths) in a third of the bars.
5. Colour: a slide into each phrase's first note; one whole-step bend on a held note every 2 bars, a little out of tune.
6. Noisy mode (or the noisy half of a mixed solo): bursts of 8-16 notes near the top of the range with chromatic clusters, then big two-string bends of 1.5-2 steps released slowly, timing loose.
7. Resolve each 4-bar phrase to the chord's root; a narrow, slow vibrato only on the last note.
8. Stop at 8 bars (16 at most); no whammy, no dives, no fast sequences, no tapping.

## Notes on the sources
- The LickLibrary pages are course descriptions in a marketing voice; their In Bloom text is the most concrete source (pentatonic, chromatic passing tones, riff-based, double stops and bends, root resolution) and is kept, but it is one lesson writer's reading of one solo.
- "Ironic solos" is Billy Corgan's opinion of a peer; "sarcastic", "massacre" are a MusicRadar writer's description of one solo. Kept as character, not as rules.
- Search summaries claimed lessons on Lithium's solo ("bends and slides") and Come As You Are's ("double stops"); those pages weren't read, so they aren't used.
- His own words on lead playing are almost absent: the 1992 Guitar World interview is gear and attitude ("musically and rhythmically retarded"), plus the "less than you could possibly imagine" line quoted by MusicRadar.
- Satriani, Vai, Bettencourt and Novoselic praise his rhythm, pocket and ear for a hook, not his leads; useful for the rhythm rule only.
- The guitar used on Heart-Shaped Box is disputed (Albini vs Rash); irrelevant to MIDI.
