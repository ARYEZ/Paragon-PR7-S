# Slash: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no article and stay [I]. Lesson examples are the writers' own licks "in the style"; only habits they state as Slash's are kept.

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic is the core: "the minor pentatonic remains at the core of his playing" while he "often dips into harmonic minor" [S, GW 10 approaches]; a solo that is "a masterclass in F# pentatonics", "the pentatonic framework that's always been a big part of his musical DNA" [S, GW Best solos]. His own words: "I know how to play major, minor and pentatonic scales all the way up the neck, but that's about as complicated as I get"; "take a basic scale and change the notes around to suit my needs" [S, GW Dear Guitar Hero].
- The blues scale's b5 as spice: "the occasional flat five adding some bluesy spice", "flat fives to add to its overall bluesiness" [S, GW Best solos]; pentatonic bars extending into the blues scale [S, GW 10 approaches].
- Natural minor colour: "minor sixths for an Aeolian feel" over minor pentatonic [S, GW Best solos]; a slow melodic section "built around the... minor scale with a few major 7ths thrown in for a harmonic minor flavor" [S, GW Best solos]. Major 6ths "for a Dorian flavor" in a fast legato outro [S, GW Best solos]; Dorian double stops in a recent track [S, GW 10 approaches].
- Harmonic minor runs up one string [S, GW 10 approaches, MR 50 licks]; harmonic minor runs with "more in common with the flamenco masters" than neoclassical playing [S, MR Emotional content]. Phrygian: a solo starting on the minor second and resolving a fret down over a chord move a half step down; he: "I just know the note is a half-step away from where it would normally be" [S, GW Best solos, MR Emotional content].
- Major/minor: "effortlessly switches between minor and major pentatonic shapes, much like the original blues greats" [S, GW Best solos]; a favourite shape that switches "between major pentatonic and pure major scale phrasing" [S, GW 10 approaches]. Over happy-sounding major chords: "I still think in blues. I just don't put the minor thirds in; I try to stick with the major... an old-school, 12-bar BB King feel" [S, MR Emotional content].
- Chord-led: "You figure out what the chord changes are going to be and then just do whatever you feel"; "changing a couple of notes around the chord changes" [S, GW 2025]. Arpeggio-based phrasing and diminished runs in a few late-'90s solos, brought back to pentatonics; sweep picking "very rarely" [S, GW Best solos]. He doesn't want "to just play scales or some cool arpeggiated technique" [S, MR Emotional content]. [I] Default minor pentatonic; over a major chord major pentatonic without the b3; natural minor's b6 and harmonic minor's 7 in melodic/dramatic sections about one phrase in four; chord tones on phrase starts and held notes.

## 2. Density and space
- "It's all about emotion to me. I can sit on one note forever"; not a speed picker ("I'm not that great a right-hand picker") [S, GW 1992]. Minimalist ballad solos, "milking each and every note with slow bends and expressive vibrato", faster runs added in the final solo [S, GW Best solos]; "a few choice bends say more with less" [S, GW Best solos].
- Fast when the energy calls for it: "When I play fast, it really is an energy thing... you're just really aggressive and you're emoting" [S, GW Energy]; "It's not that I don't like playing fast, because I do", especially after playing rhythm a while [S, GW Appetite]. He dislikes players "in one hand position... going as fast as they can" [S, GW Appetite].
- [I] Melodic sections 2-6 notes a bar with a held note of 1-4 beats every bar or two; bursts 12-24 notes (legato 16ths, triplets, 32nd flurries) for at most a bar or two; ballads stay at the low end.

## 3. Phrasing
- Two-part solos: a melodic, "almost Spanish acoustic-sounding splash of neatly connected melodic ideas" on the neck pickup, then a "frenzied" second half on the bridge pickup [S, GW Appetite, GW Best solos]; a ballad's last solo adds faster runs [S, GW Best solos]. [I] First half melodic and sparse, second half denser and higher.
- Solos as melodies of the song: "guitar solos as being really important to the song as far as melody is concerned" [S, GW Living the Dream]; "it means something in the context of the song; it's not just a ripping solo" [S, GW 2025]; "finding melodies that fit the whole thing" [S, MR Emotional content]. He hears a phrase before playing it [S, GW Appetite].
- Sequences: "a four-note pattern, repeating each time one note farther up the scale", used "frequently" [S, GW 10 approaches]. A motif stated, then varied an octave higher up the neck [S, GW Best solos]. Openings with a bend "held and released several times" [S, GW Best solos], or a "screaming double stop on the two higher strings" [S, GW Best solos].
- Improvised, then kept: solos "very spontaneous and at-the-moment"; live he sticks with "what I came up with spontaneously the first time round", changing "little notes here and there" [S, GW 2025, MR Emotional content]; trading phrases with a second guitarist, fast against bluesy [S, MR Emotional content]. [I] A motif, an answer, the motif varied higher, a run, a resolving held note.

## 4. Rhythm
- Loose, "behind-the-beat attitude", "it's all about feel" [S, GW 10 approaches]; bends played "with a typically loose, drawling style... not too tight" [S, MR 5 ways]. [I] Melodic notes 10-30 ms late, runs nearer the grid.
- Constant eighth-note lines [S, GW 10 approaches]; a three-note motif against four notes a beat (displacement) as "a classic Slash-style move" [S, MR 25 blues-rock]; double-picked descending notes [S, GW Best solos].
- Legato flurries of 32nds and triplets between main melody notes [S, MR 5 ways]; rapid pull-off/hammer-on outros [S, GW Best solos].

## 5. Bends
- Bends are central: "slow bends" in ballads [S, GW Best solos]; "fluid string bending" with "correct pitching" [S, MR 5 ways]; oblique bends (a held note ringing against a bent one) [S, MR 5 ways]; unison bends and double-stop bends [S, LL Quick Licks]; high-register bends [S, MR 5 ways]; "savage, atonal bends" live [S, GW Appetite]; he listens for "every... overbend or underbend" [S, MR Emotional content]. A bend held and released several times [S, GW Best solos].
- [I] Whole step 60%, half step 25%, 1.5 steps 15%; bends often slow (a quarter to half a beat to reach pitch); 1-2 bends a bar in melodic sections; bend-release-rebend on long notes.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Expressive vibrato" on held notes [S, GW Best solos]; "wide, controlled, and emotive" [S, LL Quick Licks: a course blurb, weak evidence].
- [I] Wide (+/-0.4-0.5 semitone), about 5-6 Hz, on notes of a beat or more, starting about 25% into the note; on top of bends too.

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato: hammer-ons and pull-offs between main melody notes [S, MR 5 ways]; legato-based fast outros [S, GW Best solos]; legato and slides [S, LL].
- Double stops ("I know most chord voicings, doublestops") [S, GW Appetite]; Dorian diads [S, GW 10 approaches].
- Tapping: "I can't tap to save my life"; only a blues lick tapped an octave up, never recorded [S, GW 1992]. A high trill he dropped because he couldn't play it consistently [S, GW 1992]. Slide: "not a good slide player", thick chords, generic licks [S, GW 1992].
- Wah (a solo's second half) and talk box: out of scope. Pickup switching between halves of a solo [S, GW Appetite]: out of scope.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Wah, talk box, pickup switching (neck for the melodic half, bridge for the wild half), the Les Paul/Marshall tone, slide, whale noises with delay and the volume knob, bends past the profile's range, pick attack.

## 9. Recipe
1. Minor pentatonic in the key; b5 as a passing spice; over a major chord major pentatonic with no b3.
2. Open with a held, slowly bent note released and re-bent, or a high double stop; wide vibrato.
3. First half (melodic): 2-6 notes a bar, chord tones on the bar's first beat, a held note every bar or two; natural minor b6 or harmonic minor 7 for drama about one phrase in four.
4. State a short motif, answer it, then play it again an octave or a position higher.
5. Run: a four-note sequence climbing one scale step each time, or a three-note motif in 16ths across the beat.
6. Second half (energy): legato flurries (32nds, triplets) between melody notes, constant eighths, oblique or unison bends; at most two bars of speed in a row.
7. Feel: melodic notes slightly behind the beat; bends slow and a little loose.
8. End on a long bent or vibratoed chord tone, resolving; the last bar mostly one note.

## Notes on the sources
- Slash describes himself as untechnical and feel-first; most specific note choices come from writers' analyses (GW Best solos, GW 10 approaches), not from him. His own words cover: pentatonic/major/minor only, no minor 3rd over major chords, the half step, spontaneity, hearing it first, speed as energy.
- The lesson examples (GW 10 approaches, MR 5 ways, MR 50 licks, MR 25 blues-rock) are writers' licks; only the stated habits are kept. LickLibrary pages are course blurbs (vibrato, unison bends): weak evidence.
- Era: diminished/arpeggio phrasing and rare sweeps belong to a few Snakepit solos; harmonic minor and Phrygian to a few dramatic GN'R solos; the default is pentatonic blues.
- GW "Five Slash licks" was a video page with no text; GW 1990, the Apocalyptic Love interview and the blues-festival interview had nothing on soloing; Premier Guitar's interview didn't load. Influences (Jeff Beck "note for note", Aerosmith, Zeppelin) are from GP Sold my soul.
