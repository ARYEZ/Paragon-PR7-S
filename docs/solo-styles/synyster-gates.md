# Synyster Gates: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no article and stay [I]. The evidence for his note choice is thin: the scale names come from LickLibrary's course and song-lesson descriptions (Andy James's licks "in the style"), not from Gates; what he says himself is about melody, harmony lines, picking and jazz.

## 1. Note choice
- Harmonic minor and Phrygian dominant: LickLibrary's song lesson names both in one of his solos, "particularly during the solo sections" (D minor song) [S, LL Beast]; its style course lists harmonic minor, Phrygian dominant and diminished as his "exotic scales" [S, LL Quick Licks]. Neither is Gates's own word. [I] Natural minor as home, harmonic minor's raised 7th over the V and at peaks, Phrygian dominant over a major chord a half step above or a major V, for a phrase or two at a time.
- Gypsy jazz and minor blues: a title-track solo "based on minor blues changes", Django's technique "applied to metal"; "people... think that it's neo-classical, but it's really gypsy jazz" [S, MR HTTK]. Gypsy jazz gave him "the technique, the scales, just the whole nuance" [S, GW Gypsy jazz]; studied fusion and bebop at MI/GIT [S, GW Wicked]; Kessel, Pass, Gambale, Robben Ford, Holdsworth [S, GW Chops]. [I] Jazz colour = chord-tone arpeggios with chromatic approach notes a half step under the target, a minor 6th (Dorian/melodic minor) over minor chords.
- Chord-led, theory-aware: over a minor chord moving a half step to a major chord he thinks of the major chord as the V's third or brings in its own third, "the less apparent route" [S, GW The Stage]. "Whole tone and augmented... all over the place", "tension and resolution", "invent your own scales" [S, MR Hang-ups]. [I] Target the third of each new chord on its first beat; one whole-tone or augmented run at most per solo, resolving to a chord tone.
- Diminished: only the course's list [S, LL Quick Licks]. [I] A diminished arpeggio over a dominant or as a passing bar, at most once in 8 bars.
- "Nonstop semitones" is a fan's description in a question he didn't contradict [S, GW DGH, weak]; fast chromatic runs [S, LL Beast]. [I] Chromatic passing notes on off-beats in fast runs.

## 2. Density and space
- Two modes he names himself: slow and melodic on ballads ("I don't want to play fast"), and sections where he blows heads off; he is proudest of slowing down; "even if it's fast, it's gotta be tasteful" [S, GW Chops, GW Wicked].
- The songs decide: "I wanted the songs to influence my playing rather than me imposing a signature style" [S, MR HTTK]. Later albums aimed to "create space instead of just filling every song up to the brim" [S, GW HTTK]; the band admits past guitars were "too busy" [S, GW The Stage, Vengeance].
- [I] Ballad mode 3-6 notes a bar with held notes of 1-2 beats; shred mode 16ths, sextuplets and 32nds (16-32 a bar) for 1-4 bars; a solo of 8-16 bars, one guitar-led "bridge" solo may run 16-32.

## 3. Phrasing
- Melody first: "melody above all else... the number-one thing" [S, GW Chops]; his strength is "composing solos that capture the energy and spirit of a song" [S, MR Hang-ups]. Slash "the best melodic player of all time" [S, GW Chops].
- A solo can take the vocal bridge's place "and take you places" [S, MR HTTK]; call-and-response with the singer [S, GW Chops].
- Arc: "something chaotic... a machine-gun spray that would build into something more melodic" (one solo) [S, MR HTTK]. [I] Either chaos into melody, or melody into a shred climax; pick one per solo.
- Improvised first, then kept: "all of my solos were improvised initially" [S, MR HTTK]; written "way over my head" in the studio [S, GW DGH].
- [I] Phrases of 2 bars; a motif stated, answered a third or sixth away (see harmony), developed into a fast run.

## 4. Rhythm
- Fast alternate picking, economy picking (his "secret"), sweeps [S, MR Hang-ups, LL Quick Licks, GW Gypsy jazz]. An arpeggio section alternate-picked, not swept (a fan-guitarist's remark) [S, GW Tailgunner, weak].
- Gypsy-jazz swing in the title-track intro [S, MR HTTK]. [I] One swung or triplet-feel bar per solo where the song allows; shred runs straight on the grid, 16ths or sextuplets, starting on a beat.

## 5. Bends
- "Chainsaw-like octave bends" in a recent song's opening [S, GW Nobody]; whammy bar use [S, LL Quick Licks]; he needs a Floyd Rose and "can throw [it] all over the place" [S, MR Contemporary, GW DGH].
- [I] Whole step 60%, half step 25%, 1.5 steps 10%, octave (12 semitones, by whammy/bend range) 5% as an opening or closing gesture; 1 bend a bar in melodic sections, none in runs.

## 6. Vibrato
- No source describes it. [I] Moderate width (+/-0.4 semitone) about 6 Hz on notes of a beat or more, from 30% in; ballads wider and later.

## 7. Other techniques
- Sweep-picked arpeggios: "warp-speed sweeps", "lightning-quick arpeggios" [S, GW Nobody]; sweeping since his teens [S, GW Wicked]. [I] 3-5 string triads or seventh chords of the bar's chord, up and down, 16th triplets or sextuplets, 1-2 bars per solo.
- Wide-interval licks across octaves [S, LL Quick Licks]. [I] Leaps of a 6th or more in a run, once a phrase in shred mode.
- Harmonised twin leads, in his own words: he plays the harmony part; "I don't like using fourths and fifths"; thirds (major and minor, diatonic) above the melody, then dropped an octave so the harmony is sixths below [S, GW DGH]. Vengeance recalls him saying a dual line was "just a fifth above" [S, MR Hang-ups]: so fifths happen, but his stated taste is thirds/sixths. Live harmonisers jump "from a sixth above to a sixth below", major or minor third by chord [S, MR Hang-ups]. Influences: the Eagles' "Hotel California", Boston, Petrucci with keys [S, GW Wicked]; harmonies used like strings [S, GW The Stage].
- Legato: "the worst thing in the world for me... my left hand can't do it" [S, MR Hang-ups]. [I] Little: slurs only on 2-note pairs; no long legato runs.
- Tapping in the course [S, LL Quick Licks]; he says he might "suck at tapping" [S, MR Hang-ups]. [I] Rare.
- Wah rarely [S, MR HTTK]; slide rarely [S, GW Chops]. Out of scope.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Whammy dives and octave scoops beyond the bend range, the Sustainiac and compressor sustain, intelligent harmonisers, wah, slide, nylon/gypsy guitar tone, pick attack.

## 9. Recipe
1. Natural minor of the key; harmonic minor's raised 7th over V chords and at the peak; Phrygian dominant for one phrase over a major chord a half step above the tonic or a major V.
2. Choose the arc: ballad (melody throughout, 3-6 notes a bar) or chaos into melody / melody into shred.
3. Open with a 2-bar vocal-like motif, chord tones (the third) on each chord's first beat, a held note with vibrato.
4. Answer it, optionally as a harmony: the same line a diatonic third above or sixth below (never fourths), as a second voice if the engine has one.
5. Develop into a run: 16ths or sextuplets alternate-picked, chromatic passing notes on off-beats, one wide leap.
6. A swept arpeggio of the chord under the bar (1-2 bars), up and back; at most one diminished or whole-tone flourish, resolving to a chord tone.
7. Optionally a swung or triplet bar (the gypsy-jazz colour) with half-step approaches to chord tones.
8. End melodic: a held bent note (a whole step) on the root or third of the last chord, or an octave bend/dive if the bend range allows.

## Notes on the sources
- His own statements (melody first, songs first, thirds/sixths harmonies, economy picking, weak legato, gypsy jazz and minor blues, chord-led note choice) are from Guitar World and MusicRadar interviews and are strong.
- The scales (harmonic minor, Phrygian dominant, diminished) are only in LickLibrary's marketing descriptions of Andy James's lessons; plausible but not his words. No source gave Gates's own view of these scales.
- "Nonstop semitones, crazy arpeggios, four notes on one string" is a reader's question in GW DGH; Gates neither confirms nor denies.
- The Tailgunner remark (alternate-picked arpeggios) is another guitarist's observation.
- Nothing found on his vibrato, bend sizes or how often he bends; those numbers are all [I]. Premier Guitar's Rig Rundown page returned no text.
