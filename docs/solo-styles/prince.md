# Prince: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. He gave very few guitar interviews and would not name influences or talk gear, so most evidence is bandmates' memories and writers' descriptions of records; there is no technical lesson on his solos in these sources beyond one Guitar World study. Notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no article and stay [I].

## 1. Note choice
- Pentatonic rock is the core: a bandmate who took the "theory" solos says Prince did "crazy pentatonic stuff like he'd been doing forever" [S, GP WMGGW (Mike Scott)]; "when Prince went off on a pentatonic rock solo" [S, MR 5 things]; "pentatonic licks" after the noise on U Got the Look [S, GW 15 best].
- Major and minor pentatonic mixed: phrases "mostly based around shape 1, but visualise it more as Major Pentatonic with 'blue' notes thrown in - notably the b3 and the b7" [S, GW Secrets: the writer's study, said to show how he combines them]. [I] Over major chords: major pentatonic with the b3 and b7 as passing/blue notes (b3 bent or slid up to 3); over minor chords: minor pentatonic.
- Two sources of colour, named by everyone: Santana's melodic side (Purple Rain; a Santana medley closing his 1994 set; Joy in Repetition "goes full Carlos Santana") and Hendrix's "fiery pentatonic blues-rock" [S, GW Secrets, GW 1994, GW 15 best]; he loved early Santana records [S, GP 2000, MR 5 things]. Chuck Berry on one solo ("I tried to go after Chuck Berry") [S, GP 2000].
- Outside, on purpose: solos that "fling themselves violently outside the confines of traditional blues riffing", a second solo of "mega-distorted, dissonant madness" [S, GW 1994]; "experiments with atonality and dissonance" [S, GW 15 best (Electric Chair)]; "unholy squeals and atonal moans" before the pentatonic licks [S, GW 15 best (U Got the Look)]; "mysterious modes, abrupt key changes" on an instrumental [S, GW 15 best]. [I] At most one dissonant/chromatic outburst a solo, at the peak, then back into the pentatonic.
- Vocal sources: "Guitarists should listen to singers for solo ideas - especially women singers... Try to play one of the runs that Beyoncé or Ella Fitzgerald does" [S, GP 2004]; his "wild high notes" were "an extension of his voice or singing" [S, PG Wendy]. [I] Melodic phrases shaped like a sung line: stepwise, a run down from a held high note.
- Chord tones: [I] chord tones on held notes and phrase ends; on the funk side the band's lessons use Dorian (minor I to major IV) for rhythm parts [S, MR Funk, rhythm only].

## 2. Density and space
- His own rule, told to a faster sideman: "all those notes don't mean nothing because they don't translate in an arena. I'll hold a high note and have people screaming while you play 100 notes" [S, GP Band (Mike Scott)]. "He understood the power of holding a sustained note" [S, MR 5 things].
- But he could shred: "an absolute shred monster" live [S, GP 2004]; "speedy precision of his runs" [S, GW 15 best]; "16th-note flurries" in a transition [S, GW 15 best (Computer Blue)]; "speedy runs with hummable repeated themes" [S, GW 15 best (Purple Rain)].
- [I] Melodic stretches 2-5 notes a bar with one held high note (1-2 beats) every 2 bars; flurries of 12-16 notes a bar (16ths, some 16th triplets) for 1-2 bars at the build; 16-32 bars for an outro solo.

## 3. Phrasing
- Improvised: asked whether his solos are planned or spontaneous: "Spontaneous" [S, GW 1994]; the Purple Rain solo made up live on the night [S, MR Purple Rain].
- The build: "begun... in a restrained manner. Eeking out subtle bends and proto-licks", then "built pace as each bar progressed with gradually rising flurries of energy", a home motif "to which he could return as he soared into the higher reaches of the neck" [S, MR Purple Rain]. Restraint to "onslaughts of full-blooded power" [S, MR Purple Rain]; passion "escalated into a full-on scream" in a final stretch [S, GW 15 best (The Morning Papers)].
- A short motif that comes back: "hummable repeated themes" [S, GW 15 best]; the "rotating central motif" [S, MR Purple Rain]. [I] A 1-2 bar motif stated early, returned to 2-3 times, each time an octave or a position higher.
- Endings: "a risky final note" [S, GW 15 best]; [I] end on a held high note with vibrato, or a high bend held to the bar line.
- Harmony lines: "massed guitar harmonies" [S, GW 15 best (Electric Chair)]; he asked a sideman to "do a harmony" to what he played [S, GP Band]. [I] Optional: a doubled phrase a diatonic third above.

## 4. Rhythm (his funk feel)
- Rhythm first: "a lot of cats don't work on their rhythm enough... The next thing is pitch... When you get these things down, then you can learn how to solo" [S, GP 2004].
- Short notes: "It's all about rhythmic precision and the attack on your notes. He preferred to keep it extremely short... thinking about the notes having no value... that's how short and tight they are" [S, MR Grantis, on his funk licks]. "His picking hand was everything" [S, PG Wendy].
- Triplets came naturally to him: the doubled triplet lick on Computer Blue was "just Prince's on perfect display... a part that he played so naturally" [S, PG Wendy].
- On stage he took "the rock thing" and gave the funk and jazz to the other guitarist [S, MR Levi]. [I] So the solo engine plays two modes: a funk mode (short, staccato 16th-note stabs on the grid, gate 30-50% of the step, accents on the "e" and "a") for fills between vocal lines or a funk section, and a rock mode for the solo proper (legato, held notes).
- [I] 16ths straight in funk mode; triplets and 16th triplets in rock flurries.

## 5. Bends
- "A drooling construction of escalating bends" [S, GW 15 best (Bambi)]; "subtle bends" at a solo's start [S, MR Purple Rain]; "double-stop screeches" (on a riff) [S, GW 15 best]; double-stop bends "with real authority and passion" in the writer's study of his melodic side [S, GW Secrets]. Bends listed among the Purple Rain solo's techniques [S, LL Purple Rain].
- [I] Escalating: a phrase that bends the same note further each time (half step, whole step, 1.5 steps). Sizes: whole step 55%, half step 30%, 1.5-2 steps 15% (the high climax notes). 1 bend a bar in melodic stretches, none inside flurries; a double-stop bend (a whole step on the lower note, the upper held) about once in 8 bars.

## 6. Vibrato
- "A Santana-level vibrato" [S, MR 5 things]; "lusty vibrato" [S, GW 15 best]; his long-time bassist and teacher: "A lot of people say he sounds like Hendrix; but to me, he doesn't really. His vibrato is different. Just the way he attacks the guitar is different" [S, GW 1994, GW Sonny T]. Vibrato named in the Purple Rain lesson [S, LL Purple Rain].
- [I] On every held note of a beat or more: about +/-0.4 semitone at 5-6 Hz, starting 20% into the note, deepening toward the end (Santana-like); none on funk-mode notes.

## 7. Other techniques
- Pinched artificial harmonics, "cascades" of runs [S, GW 15 best (I'm Yours)]; "full-neck skids" (slides along the neck) on a riff [S, GW 15 best]; slides, double stops and string skipping in the Purple Rain lesson [S, LL Purple Rain].
- "Adrian Belew-like bird squeals" and feedback-drenched noise with "blurred-pick-hand frenzy" [S, GW 15 best]: effects and feedback, mostly out of scope.
- Clean and distorted contrast: on tour he told a sideman to take a clean solo after his distortion one [S, GP Band]. [I] Not a note habit; skip.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
His tone (Hohner Tele copy, Boss DS-1/DS-2 into a Mesa/Boogie: "voltage through the board"), the flanger on solos (Let's Go Crazy, Purple Rain live), the octaver, wah, Leslie, Rotovibe, envelope filter, feedback and squeals, keyboards doubling guitar lines, panning, the pick hidden between fingers for slapped funk rhythm [S, GW LGC, GP 2000, GP 2004, GW Dez, PG Wendy, GW Grantis PR].

## 9. Recipe
1. Scale: major pentatonic with b3 and b7 blue notes over major chords, minor pentatonic over minor; chord tones on held notes and phrase ends.
2. Open restrained: 2-4 bars of few notes, small bends (half step) and a vocal-shaped phrase, lots of space.
3. State a short (1-2 bar) motif; bring it back 2-3 times, each a position or octave higher.
4. Between returns, a held high note (1-2 beats) with deep, slowish vibrato (+/-0.4 semitone, 5-6 Hz), "the note that makes them scream".
5. Escalate: the same note bent further each time (half, whole, 1.5 steps); a double-stop bend now and then.
6. Build: rising flurries (16ths, 16th triplets, a triplet lick) for 1-2 bars, getting longer as the solo goes on.
7. Peak: one outburst outside the key (chromatic or dissonant) or a pinch harmonic, then straight back to the pentatonic.
8. End on a high held or bent note with vibrato, the motif's last return. In funk sections instead: short staccato 16th stabs, tight to the grid, no vibrato.

## Notes on the sources
- Prince's own words on guitar are few: rhythm then pitch then soloing, listen to women singers (GP 2004), solos spontaneous, favourite guitarist Sonny T (GW 1994), Chuck Berry on one solo and Santana's early albums (GP 2000). Everything else is bandmates (Mike Scott, Wendy Melvoin, Sonny Thompson, Donna Grantis, Levi Seacer Jr, Dez Dickerson) and writers.
- The Guitar World "soloing secrets" studies are the writer's own music; only the stated habits are kept (major pentatonic with b3/b7, melody and sustain, double-stop bends).
- Donna Grantis' chromatic approach notes, fourths shifted a semitone and holding a b5 (MusicRadar) are her own, from her album interview, not said of Prince: left out. Her "notes having no value" is about his funk licks.
- The MusicRadar funk lesson's examples (power-chord stabs, Dorian chords, syncopated pentatonic) are rhythm parts in the writer's own words; used only for the Dorian note under rhythm.
- The LickLibrary page is a lesson description (bends, vibrato, slides, double stops, string skipping); weak evidence.
- Dez Dickerson says that in the early 1980s Prince thought Dez the better lead player; after Wendy (not a lead player) joined, "Prince kind of took that role back" and grew as a guitarist (GW Dez). The style above is mostly from Purple Rain on.
- Mike Scott's "crazy pentatonic stuff" was said in contrast to his own theory-heavy solos, so it is evidence for pentatonic, not against outside moments (which the record reviews describe).
