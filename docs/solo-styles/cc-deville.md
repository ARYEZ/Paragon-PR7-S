# C.C. DeVille: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. **The evidence is thin.** No interview in which he explains how he solos was found on the allowed sites; the one real source on his lead playing is a Guitar World feature describing his solos song by song. The LickLibrary pages are lesson descriptions in a marketing voice and say what techniques a transcription contains, not why. Everything about scales, notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often is [I], drawn from those few descriptions and the glam/hard-rock idiom they place him in. Treat this profile as lighter-weight than the others.

## 1. Note choice
- Blues-rooted hard rock: "bluesy roots with glam metal flair" [S, LL Nothin]; "elements of bluesy rock, classic rock 'n' roll, and heavy metal" [S, LL Unskinny]; "classic rock, glam metal, and blues" [S, LL Rose]. Nashville/country licks on acoustic, a Page-like country-blues reworking [S, GW Moments].
- Melody first: "exceedingly melodic bursts of speed" [S, GW Moments]; "melodic solos", "melodic phrasing", "vocal-like phrasing" in the ballads [S, LL Rose, LL Nothin].
- Outside moments: by 1990 one solo "suggests... C.C. was becoming a fan of Vernon Reid's avant-metal freak-outs" [S, GW Moments]. [I] One chromatic/outside burst per solo at most, in an uptempo song, resolving to a chord tone.
- [I] Minor pentatonic and blues scale (with the b5) over rock songs; major pentatonic / Mixolydian colour over the major I-IV-V rock'n'roll changes his riffs sit on (sus4 chords [S, GW Moments]); chord tones on held notes and phrase ends, the sus4 (the 4th resolving to the 3rd) as a target.

## 2. Density and space
- Sustain first, speed last: a solo that "begins by riding a few sustained notes, saving the burst of speed for the last possible moment" [S, GW Moments]; "drawn-out bends interspersed with... bursts of speed" [S, GW Moments].
- Ballad solos built on "long, languorous bends" (compared with Gilmour) [S, GW Moments].
- [I] Opening bars 2-5 notes a bar (held bends, double stops); bursts 12-16 notes a bar (16ths, some sextuplets), one or two bursts a solo, the biggest at the end; solos 8-16 bars, ballads 8.

## 3. Phrasing
- Composed, song-serving solos: "his attention to composition carries over to his solo" [S, GW Moments]; "flashy but musical", "technique can serve the song while still delivering jaw-dropping moments" [S, LL Nothin]; "memorable solos that enhance the song's emotional weight" [S, LL Rose].
- Brian May-like phrasing: long bends alternating with melodic fast runs [S, GW Moments]. [I] Phrase pairs: a held bend (1-2 beats) answered by a short melodic run, 2-4 times, then the closing burst.
- Double-stop melodies: "the lyrical assortment of double-stops in his solo" [S, GW Moments]; double stops "to thicken the melody lines" [S, LL Talk Dirty, LL Rose].
- Showpiece: live, his solo spot ends with a burst of "Eruption" as a tribute to Van Halen [S, MR Eruption]. [I] Tapping as a rare closing flourish, not in song solos.

## 4. Rhythm
- "Keen sense of rhythm"; lines "delightfully unpredictable when they go against the beat" [S, GW Moments].
- Fast passages alternate-picked [S, LL Fallen Angel, LL Nothin].
- [I] Displaced phrases: start runs on an off-beat 16th or the "and" of a beat; a three- or six-note figure repeated across the bar line; held notes on beats 1 and 3.

## 5. Bends
- Bends throughout: "expressive bends" [S, LL Nothin]; string bending in every lesson [S, LL Rose, LL Fallen Angel, LL Unskinny]; pre-bends "to create tension and release" [S, LL Unskinny]; "drawn-out", "long, languorous" bends [S, GW Moments].
- [I] Whole step 65%, half step 25%, 1.5 steps 10%; pre-bend and release about once a solo; slow bends (reaching pitch over a quarter to half a beat) in ballads, quicker in rockers; about one bend a bar in sustained sections.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Signature use of bends, vibrato"; solos "filled with vibrato" on sustained notes [S, LL Rose]; vibrato on held notes [S, LL Unskinny, LL Nothin].
- [I] Medium width (+/-0.3-0.5 semitone) at about 5-6 Hz on notes of a beat or more; on the top of held bends.

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato: hammer-ons and pull-offs for "fluid, legato runs" [S, LL Rose, LL Unskinny, LL Fallen Angel]; a hit built on a busy hammer-on/pull-off riff [S, GW Moments].
- Slides between notes "adding a singing quality" [S, LL Rose, LL Nothin, LL Unskinny].
- Pinch harmonics: "his solos often include pinched harmonics" [S, LL Unskinny, LL Nothin].
- Whammy: "explosive whammy bar tricks" [S, LL Nothin], dives [S, LL Unskinny]. Pick slides and scrapes, one "of the longest in recorded history" [S, GW Moments, LL Unskinny].

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Pick scrapes, whammy dives past the bend range, the "big-ass tone" [GW Moments], stage showmanship. Pinch harmonics only as Shreddage's velocity layer.

## 9. Recipe
1. Key's minor pentatonic / blues scale over minor or riff-based songs; major pentatonic with the 4th over major rock'n'roll changes; chord tones on held notes.
2. Open with 2-4 bars of sustained notes: slow whole-step bends held with vibrato, or a melodic line in double stops (thirds/fourths on adjacent strings).
3. Answer each held bend with a short melodic run (4-8 notes, legato with slides), entering off the beat.
4. Once in the middle, place a phrase against the beat: a short figure repeated so it crosses the bar line.
5. Ballads: stay in step 2-3 throughout, longer bends, fewer notes; skip step 7's speed.
6. Colour sparingly: one pre-bend and release, one pinch harmonic on a phrase end, at most one chromatic/outside burst.
7. Save the fast burst for the last 1-2 bars: picked 16ths or sextuplets down the scale.
8. End on a held bend or chord tone with vibrato on the downbeat.

## Notes on the sources
- GW Moments is a staff list piece with a jokey tone, but its comments on the solos (sustain then speed, Brian May phrasing, Gilmour bends, double stops, playing against the beat, the Vernon Reid influence) are specific to his playing and are the backbone here.
- The LickLibrary pages list techniques found in note-for-note transcriptions (a fair sign they are on the records) wrapped in generic praise; their adjectives are weak evidence. "Every Rose", "Something to Believe In" and other slugs beyond five songs were not found.
- MR Eruption is news of a live tribute, not of his own solos; used only for the showpiece. GW Matt Smith and GW Saraceno say nothing about his lead technique ("glammier direction", "a huge part of the sound") and are not used for habits.
- No interview with his own words on soloing was found; Wikipedia and Louder were unreachable.
