# Jerry Cantrell: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no article and stay [I]. The evidence is thinner than for the shredders: he says himself he isn't "a big soloist", and most of what he says is about approach, not notes. Lesson examples are the writers' own licks "in the style"; only habits they state as Cantrell's are kept.

## 1. Note choice
- By ear, not by scale: "I couldn't tell you what notes I'm playing or what scale I'm using... I just kind of fumble around until I find it" [S, GW Rainier Fog]; he keeps himself "kind of stupid on purpose" so he has "to fight through it and find it" [S, PG Playing Dumb]; "no guides, no grids" [S, GW Brighten TG].
- Blues at the root: "lyrical pentatonic lines" [S, MR Play like]; "traditional blues phrasing" [S, MR Play like]; rock and roll "a mix of blues and country", country "always an ingredient" [S, GW Brighten TG, GW Brighten AIC]; Southern, Skynyrd-like phrasing in a recent solo, which he confirms [S, GW Rainier Fog]. [I] Minor pentatonic in the key as home, the major 3rd and 6th borrowed over major chords (country/Southern colour) about one phrase in four.
- Dissonance: "dissonant harmony" and the Phrygian mode are "a big feature" (said of riffs) [S, MR Play like]; "dissonant, stabbing" riffs [S, GW Rainier Fog]. [I] In solos, the b2 and b5 as passing or bent-into tones over the riff's Phrygian moves, not as targets.
- Influences he names: Hendrix, Iommi, Page, Brian May, Lindsey Buckingham, Davey Johnstone, Robin Trower [S, GW 1996]; for simplicity and feel Johnstone, Buckingham and Billy Gibbons, whose solo is "more like another vocal part of a song" [S, GW Dear Guitar Hero]; "heavily inspired by Jimi Hendrix" [S, PG Etudes (the writer's view)].
- [I] Chord tones of the chord under the bar on phrase starts and held notes, since the line is meant to "sing" like a vocal.

## 2. Density and space
- Not a shredder: "I am not a shredder. I wish I could shred, but I kind of gave up that conquest years ago" [S, GW I Want Blood]; "more vibe than technical prowess" [S, GW I Want Blood]; "I've always dug feel more", "gotten lazier... as far as my technical ability goes" [S, GW Dear Guitar Hero]; no "ripping four- or five-minute solos" [S, PG Playing Dumb].
- Short: "I just put in what needs to be there"; the 1995 album's solos "shorter and more abstract" (the interviewer, not denied) [S, GW 1996]; a reader: "You never drag out a solo" (he agrees and says why: feel) [S, GW Dear Guitar Hero]. Sometimes no solo at all, "make your guitar part of the fabric" [S, GW Brighten TG].
- Bursts exist: "speedy legato" [S, MR Play like]; "fast legato" inside slow songs [S, MR Play like].
- [I] 3-6 notes a bar in melodic phrases, one legato burst of 12-16 notes in a solo; a held note of 1-2 beats in every phrase; 8 bars usual, 4-16.

## 3. Phrasing
- The solo as a written part of the song: "I've always written my solos"; "a musical break within the song. It has to speak and have a voice"; "a part change and a different vocal line" [S, PG Playing Dumb]. "A piece of the music that needs to sing... a cross between a horn line and a vocal line" [S, GW Rainier Fog]. "I always treat my solos like a scene change. I try to make them singable" [S, GW I Want Blood].
- Built from improvised pieces: "I throw a ton of shit up against the wall, and then I pick out the best pieces and string them together", "a really powerful and complete statement in itself" [S, GW Rainier Fog]. [I] Generate several short motifs, keep the most singable 2-3, chain them; repeat the best one with a change.
- A narrative: Guitar World hears "a beginning, a middle and an end" in one of his solos that "doesn't outstay its welcome" [S, GW Hated solo (the writer)]. [I] Statement, development, a higher climax, a short resolving tail.
- Harmonised leads: "harmonised leads rather than full-blown shredding" (the interviewer), "interesting to see what I can achieve through harmonies" [S, GW I Want Blood]; harmonised solos with "almost an Allman Brothers Band feel", now and then [S, GW Brighten AIC]; "big harmonised bends at the beginning and a laid-back outro lead" in place of a solo (the interviewer, on one song) [S, GW Brighten TG]; octave melodies in leads [S, LL Rooster, LL Them Bones]. [I] A harmony line a diatonic third (sometimes sixth) above for 2-4 bars in one solo in three.
- Slides join phrases [S, LL Rooster, LL We Die Young, LL Nutshell].

## 4. Rhythm
- Nothing sourced on his lead rhythm. The band's odd metres (7/8, 6/4, 3/4 against 4/4) are riff habits [S, PG Etudes, MR Play like].
- [I] Vocal timing: phrases start after the downbeat (an eighth or a beat late), long notes on strong beats, eighths and eighth triplets in melody, sixteenths or sextuplets only in the legato burst; held notes a little behind (0-20 ms).

## 5. Bends
- His trademark, in riffs and leads: "wide note bends" (the interviewer, GW Rainier Fog and GW Dinosaurs); his words: "There's a real tortured element there when you slow bend into a note and hold it to get some sort of dissonance. It just sounds lumbering" [S, GW Dinosaurs]; what he takes from Iommi: "the heaviness, the bends" [S, GW Iommi]; "Sabbath-influenced string bends that sound like there's no pitch centre" [S, MR Play like]; a minor-to-major-3rd bend in a riff (the interviewer) [S, GW Brighten TG]; "quarter-note bending" in a riff (the interviewer) [S, GW I Want Blood interview].
- Unison bends (one string bent up to another held) in leads [S, LL Them Bones]; bends "throughout" leads [S, LL Rooster, LL Would, LL Man in the Box].
- [I] Slow bends: the rise over 1/2-1 beat (not a snap), held; sizes whole step 50%, half step 30% (the minor-to-major-3rd move), 1.5 steps 10%, a quarter tone 10% on the b3; about 1 in 4 bends left a hair flat or sharp (10-30 cents) and held against a chord tone for the "dissonance" he describes; 1-2 bends a bar in melodic phrases, none in bursts; a unison bend once a solo.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Dark, bluesy vibrato" (the interviewer) [S, GW I Want Blood interview]; "wide vibrato" [S, MR Play like, MR Dirt]; tuning down a semitone allows "more aggressive vibrato" (the writer's reason) [S, MR Play like]; vibrato on bends and sustained notes [S, LL Rooster].
- [I] Wide (+/-0.4-0.6 semitone), slowish 4.5-5.5 Hz, on notes of a beat or more, from about 30% in; on held bends too.

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato hammer-ons and pull-offs for fast and flowing lines [S, MR Play like, LL Would, LL We Die Young, LL Rooster].
- Trills [S, MR Dirt]; double stops in leads [S, LL Would]; harmonics [S, LL Rooster, LL Man in the Box].
- Wah as voice: "the one effect that I use the most", "it makes it speak"; "held wide-open, it makes the sound squashed and dark" [S, MR Tone]; half or three-quarter sweeps, not full ones [S, PG Playing Dumb]. Talk box and Rotovibe on recent records [S, GW I Want Blood, GW I Want Blood interview, GW 1996]. Out of scope.
- One lead track up the middle, two rhythm guitars hard left and right [S, GW 1996].

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Wah (his main lead sound: partial sweeps, the parked "squashed and dark" tone), talk box, Rotovibe, feedback, the half-step-down and drop-D weight, reverb and delay washes, double-tracked rhythm under the lead, pick attack.

## 9. Recipe
1. Key's minor pentatonic as home; over major chords borrow the major 3rd and 6th; the b2/b5 only as bent-into or passing tones over Phrygian riffs.
2. Write, don't noodle: make 5-6 short vocal motifs (2-4 beats, 3-6 notes a bar, chord tones on starts and held notes), keep the 2-3 most singable, chain them into one statement.
3. Enter after the downbeat; open with a slow, held whole-step or half-step bend with wide slow vibrato (optionally harmonised a third above).
4. Develop: repeat the best motif with a change (shifted up the scale, an end altered), slides joining positions.
5. Colour: one bend held slightly off pitch (10-30 cents) against a chord tone for the "tortured" dissonance; a unison bend or a short trill.
6. Climax: one legato burst (12-16 hammer-on/pull-off notes) or a higher held bend, then space.
7. Optional harmony: 2-4 bars doubled a diatonic third above (Southern/Allman feel), in one solo in three.
8. Keep it short (8 bars, never past 16) and end on a held, vibratoed chord tone, a "scene change" back into the song.

## Notes on the sources
- Most of the strong evidence is his own words on approach (written solos, singable, horn/vocal line, not a shredder, by ear). Note-level facts are few; numbers here are inference.
- Several bend and vibrato descriptions are interviewers' and writers' (Guitar World's "wide note bends", "dark, bluesy vibrato"; MusicRadar's "wide vibrato"). His own statement on bends ("slow bend into a note and hold it... dissonance") is about riffs, not solos; extending it to leads is [I].
- The Premier Guitar Electric Etudes solo (a minor-third bend, D blues scale, sextuplet legato) is the writer's lick; only "inspired by Hendrix" is kept, as the writer's view.
- The LickLibrary pages are lesson sales copy (slides, octaves, unison bends, harmonics); weak evidence. Search summaries said the We Die Young lesson names E minor pentatonic and Them Bones D minor; the fetched pages didn't say so, so it isn't used.
- A 1996 answer ("I used to... really work out something for each tune. Now, I don't think about it at all") lost its question in the page; it may be about rhythm overdubs, so it isn't used. Later interviews (2013, 2018) say he writes his solos.
- Pages read with nothing usable: GW Facelift (gear), GW Cursing (repeats Duff's quote), GW Gear EVH, GW Duff (only "Jerry fights for his leads"). Premier Guitar's rig rundown and Wikipedia weren't reached.
