# Jimmy Page: lead style (technique sheet, revised from the full articles)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. Notes per bar, bend frequency and vibrato speed are in no article and stay [I]. Many lesson examples are the writers' own licks "in the style"; only habits they state as Page's are kept.

## What changed from the first sheet
- Now sourced: bend sizes (half step to 2.5 steps, intervals named), major/minor pentatonic mixing, the natural 6th, sextuplets, six-note 16th groups and 1.5-beat licks shifting against the bar, how his bend vibrato is made, behind-the-beat playing, 32nd bursts, mid-phrase position shifts, what comes at the climax.
- Corrected: bursts play "way behind the beat at points" (Premier Guitar), not ahead; the climax is a repeated bend figure then a fast pull-off lick or descending run, not a pre-bend.
- The summaries' mistakes: "fast, even, shimmering" and "wide" vibrato come from one article (one technique, no disagreement); the "Feel over accuracy" article's wonky-vibrato examples are Kim Thayil's, not Page's.

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic, the box with the root at the 12th fret, mostly the top three or four strings [S, GW Shred Zeppelin, GW 20 Ways, MR LZ II]; the b5 often [S]; blues scale + major 7th now and then [S, GW Strategies]; the 9th as colour, the root bent up to the 9th [S, GW Shred]; the natural 6th "quite a 'Pagey' move" [S, GW Bonamassa], Dorian [S, PG]; Dorian on one-chord vamps, Lydian motifs over some chord changes [S].
- Major/minor mixing, a core habit [S]: the same box moved down three frets for major pentatonic (GW Shred); both inside one lick (GW Strategies, GW 4 Tricks); "a typical Page trick" (MR); b3 to 3 (PG); over major/dominant chords "teeters between the major third and fourth" (GW Shred). [I] Over major chords, major pentatonic in the same shape for 1-2 bars.
- Minor 3rds bent a quarter tone sharp [S, GW Ultimate]; [I] +0.25-0.5 semitone. Ascending chromatic lines mixed in [S]. Openings resolve to and outline the chord [S, PG]; [I] chord tones on downbeats and phrase ends.

## 2. Density and space
- "Crammed", "wall of notes" even in a slow blues, against "succinct, punctuated phrases" [S, GW Shred]; by 1971-73 phrasing over speed, "more unusual note groupings" [S, GW 4 Tricks]; live solos stretched (Stairway's more than doubled) [S].
- [I] Melody 4-8 notes a bar, bursts 12-24; 1-2 beats' rest; 8-16 bars in a song.

## 3. Phrasing
- "Thematic repetition", often licks cycled on two adjacent strings [S, GW Strategies]; repetition builds tension [S]; [I] 3-8 repeats (lessons: six, or "as long as you want").
- Short cells repeated climbing or falling through the scale; three-note motifs in descending sequence [S].
- Openings: a Chuck Berry "smear" (a whole-step bend 4 to 5 under a held root-5th double stop) [S, GW Shred].
- Climax: "a repeating bending figure... followed by a fast pull-off lick" [S, PG]; a final descending run and a flourish [S, GW 4 Tricks].
- Call and response with the vocal and with chord stabs [S]. Looseness: "loose as hell" at times; missed notes "part of the charm" [S]; [I] timing +/-15-25 ms.

## 4. Rhythm
- Triplet pentatonic sequences "commonplace"; triplet pull-offs three notes a beat [S]; sextuplet runs on the top two strings, staggered descending [S]; triplets mixed with straight 16ths in one flurry [S].
- Displacement [S]: 1.5-beat licks repeated across the bar; six-note 16th patterns "rotating against the 4/4 meter".
- 32nd-note picking "as fast as you can", rakes [S, PG Live]. "Way behind the beat at points" [S, PG Live]; [I] held and bent notes 10-30 ms late, bursts on the grid with jitter.

## 5. Bends
- Half and whole steps (4-b5, b3-3, 4-5, R-9); 1.5 and 2 steps (R-b3, R-3, 4-6, 5-b7); 2.5 steps up to the b6; "huge three- or four-semitone bends" [S, GW].
- Unison bends on G/B or B/E [S]; pedal-steel bends (the 3rd string bent under a held double stop) [S]; oblique bends leaving a rubbing 2nd [S]; repeated Chuck Berry bends and held bends [S]; pre-bends and bend-and-release [S]; bend, release, pull off at once [S].
- [I] Whole 55%, half 20%, 1.5-2.5 steps 20%, quarter tone 5%; 1-2 bends a bar; up in 50-120 ms.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Fast, even and shimmering", on held bends "wide" [S, GW Shred]; "wide, controlled", "wide, vocal" [S, Lick Library].
- On a bend: partly released and pushed back "in a quick, steady rhythm", so the pitch swings below the target [S, GW Shred].
- [I] About 6-7 Hz; plain notes +/-0.3 semitone; bends dip 0.5-1 semitone below the target and back.

## 7. Other techniques
- Pull-offs and hammer-ons throughout [S]; pull-offs to open strings, single and double, low licks in E and A [S]; behind-the-nut bends up to two whole steps [S]; position shifts mid-phrase joined by slides, sliding 6ths [S]; trills [S]; double stops [S].
- Register [S]: the 12th-position box home; open position for the fattest licks; several positions before high bends near the climax; a "B.B. box" round the 17th fret for big bends. [I] Path mid, low/open, high.
- Bow and theremin out of scope.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone (thinner than people assume [S, PG]), fuzz, Echoplex, wah; light strings, pick noise, rake scrape; behind-the-nut sounds beyond pitch bend; bends deeper than the bend range (2.5 steps needs +/-5 or more).

## 9. Recipe
1. Minor pentatonic in the 12th-position box; the b5, 9 and natural 6; major pentatonic in the same shape over major chords.
2. Open with a smear bend (4 to 5 over a root-5th double stop) resolving to a chord tone; fast vibrato swinging below the target; a rest.
3. Develop: short cells climbing or falling through the scale; one unison or pedal-steel bend.
4. Displace: a 1.5-beat lick or six 16ths looped 3-8 times against the bar.
5. Burst: a bar of triplet/sextuplet pull-offs or a 32nd flurry, maybe into open-string pull-offs.
6. Move up: mid-phrase slides to high over-bends of 1.5-2.5 steps.
7. Climax: a repeated bend figure.
8. Close: a fast pull-off lick or a descending run onto the root; loose, bent notes behind the beat.
