# David Gilmour: lead style (technique sheet, revised from the full articles)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo or melody of his is written out here.
**[S: source]** = an article supports it, **[I]** = inference (every notes-per-bar count, rest length and the laid-back timing are still unsourced).

## What changed from the first sheet
- Now sourced: phrases start and end on roots or 5ths; bend to the chord's 3rd, release, resolve to the root; the key's root heard as the 5th of IVm; the b5 as a passing note; the 4th added to major pentatonic (Premier Guitar). Pickups of an eighth to two beats; 8th triplets, staccato hits, the fastest burst late; groups of three against 16ths; vibrato width on a bend; how often he uses the whammy; returning motifs and a beginning-middle-end arc (Ezrin); leaps sung rather than taken from scale shapes (Gilmour); along one string and across positions.
- Corrected: vibrato on a bend dips about a quarter step below the bent pitch, "not too quickly"; he's "fussy about over-eager vibrato", sometimes only a touch.
- Old summaries' mistakes: the 1.5-step bends came from Jimmy Brown's Gilmour-style arrangement (real evidence for 3- and 4-semitone bends: Guitar World's "10 ideas", MusicRadar); the sextuplet lick is Kolb's made-up example; "vibrato from the wrist" unverified (Gilmour: fingers and whammy "in cooperation").

## 1. Note choice
- Minor pentatonic [S, all]; + major 2nd and major 6th (Dorian), both good notes to bend from [S, GW 10 ideas]; the b5 passing [S, Premier Guitar].
- Major: major pentatonic, or relative-major pentatonic + the 4th (the IV's root) [S, Premier Guitar]; the major scale over IV-I [S, GW Kolb]; Mixolydian on V, Aeolian/Dorian in minor songs [S, GW Kolb, LickLibrary].
- Chord tones [S, Premier Guitar]: roots and 5ths of I, IV, V as phrases' first/last notes; the tonic as the 5th of IVm, "a textbook Gilmour-ism"; bend up to the chord's 3rd, release, resolve to its root. Add4, add9 arpeggio shapes [S, GW Kolb].
- [I] 70-80% of held notes on chord tones; the target follows the chord.

## 2. Density and space
- Space and rests core [S, MusicRadar; Joe Perry: "what he doesn't play"; Gilmour: "just playing a tune"].
- [I] 2-6 onsets a bar early, 6-12 at the peak; 1-4 beats' rest between phrases; 30-50% rest early on.
- Arc [S]: delicate bends, climbing into higher octaves [LickLibrary]; a "declaration", then "movements", "a beginning, a middle and an ending" [Ezrin]; the fastest passage near the end and short [GW Kolb].
- Length: none fixed (live "until I think, maybe we'll end now") [S]. [I] 8-16 bars in songs, 24-32+ on long pieces.

## 3. Phrasing
- Vocal: he sings along, and sings ideas into a mic because a voice "can leap in leaps" a guitar wouldn't [S, Ezrin, Gilmour]: allow 5ths, 6ths and octaves off the scale shape.
- Motifs come back: the solo "refers back to itself several times" [S, Ezrin].
- Pickups of an eighth to two beats [S]; phrases often open on a held, repeated bend [S, GW prog-blues]; end on a held vibrato note [S, MusicRadar]. [I] 1-2 bar phrases in call and answer, the answer often higher.
- Along one string and across positions [S, MusicRadar].

## 4. Rhythm
- 8th triplets drive descending phrases; staccato notes; muted hits and some palm muting; double-stop slides across the bar line; sextuplets only in the fastest burst [S, GW Kolb, GW prog-blues].
- Groups of three over a 16th grid, of four in 12/8, for tension [S, MusicRadar]. Rakes into a bend or across a triad [S, GW Brown, GW prog-blues, Premier Guitar].
- [I] Mostly quarters, 8ths and held notes; laid back 0-30 ms (unsourced).

## 5. Bends
- "Present in almost every solo" [S, MusicRadar]. [I] 30-50% of held notes.
- Whole steps most [S, GW Kolb]; half steps over major chords [S]; quarter-tone curls, "lazy and slightly out of tune" [S, MusicRadar]; 3 semitones, 4 "if you're brave" [S, GW 10 ideas, MusicRadar].
- Two-stage: up a whole tone, ease off, on to two tones [S]; bend, hold, bend higher, release [S]; "up-down-up" (don't overuse) [S]; pre-bends and gradual releases [S]; unison bends "feature heavily" [S]; a double stop pulled slightly sharp, "one of David's favourite devices" [S].
- Full bends exactly in tune, never sharp [S]. [I] Up over 1/8-1/4 beat, slow releases.

## 6. Vibrato
- On a bent note: wide, even, release about a quarter step and back, repeated, "not too quickly" [S, GW Brown]. [I] About 4-5 Hz.
- Whammy: vibrato on bends and held notes "a lot", dips of a tone or semitone "quite frequent", wide dives "occasionally", scoops [S, GW Bishop, GW 10 ideas].
- Restraint: "fussy about over-eager vibrato"; high notes sometimes only a touch [S, Gilmour]. [I] From 20-40% into notes of a beat or more.

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato over picking; triplet pull-offs; grace-note slides into notes; double stops as "funky punctuation"; pinch harmonics occasionally, tapping rarely; slide/lap-steel glides (pitch-bend glissandi); up to 22nd-fret bends [S, MusicRadar, GW].
- [I] Double stops about 5-10% of events; about two octaves in a solo.

## 8. Out of scope
Strat pickups, fuzz, compression, stage-volume sustain; delays (about 450 ms, dotted 8ths); phaser, Uni-Vibe, rotary speaker; volume swells; picking angle and touch.

## 9. Recipe
1. Per chord: minor pentatonic + 2 and 6, or major pentatonic + 4; root or 5th targets for phrase starts and ends.
2. Arc: sparse and low, developing, high; about 3 -> 10 onsets a bar [I]; at most one short fast burst near the end [S].
3. Opening: a pickup, a held or repeated bend, a long note with vibrato.
4. Answer and develop: the same rhythm, a new ending, often higher; the opening motif back later [S].
5. Bends: mostly whole steps, b3 curls, one two-stage or multiple bend at the peak, bend to the 3rd and release to the root.
6. Colour: staccato hits, a rake, double stops, triplets, 3-against-4.
7. Vibrato: about a quarter-step dip, not fast; some high notes nearly plain.
8. End on the root or 5th, held, after a slow release.
