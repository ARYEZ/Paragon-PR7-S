# David Gilmour: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = a source supports it, **[I]** = inference (the researcher could read only search summaries: guitarworld.com and musicradar.com weren't reachable from the session).

## 1. Note choice
- Base: minor pentatonic [S]. Extra notes: the major 2nd (9th) and major 6th, a Dorian colour [S]; the 6th is the signature extra, the 2nd a passing or target note [I].
- Major passages: major pentatonic [S]; Mixolydian/modal on some tracks [S].
- [I] By the chord: major chord: major pentatonic + 2nd; minor chord: minor pentatonic + 2nd and 6th (Dorian); the b3 curled slightly sharp over major chords.
- Chord-tone targeting, "the master of target-note playing" [S]. [I] about 70-80% of held notes on chord tones (root, 3rd, 5th; 9th as colour); a phrase resolves onto the new chord's tone when it changes.
- Blues roots: B.B. King, Albert King [S].

## 2. Density and space
- "Say more with less"; he calls himself not fast, likes slow tempos [S].
- [I] 2-6 onsets a bar at the start, 6-12 at the peak; 1-4 beats of rest between phrases; rest about 30-50% of the first half.
- Arc [S]: sparse, low-to-mid on the neck, delicate bends; climbs the neck and builds; peaks with high bends.
- [I] Fast bursts (16ths, sextuplets) only in the last third, one or two beats each. 8-16 bars in songs, 32+ on epics.

## 3. Phrasing
- Vocal: a phrase is a sung line [S].
- [I] Phrases 1-2 bars; pairs make a 4-bar question and answer (the answer keeps the call's rhythm, ends differently, often higher). A motif stated, repeated with a variation, developed by moving it up.
- [I] Phrases start off the beat or with a bend; end on a long note (>= 1 beat, often 2-4) with vibrato or bend-and-hold; the last on the root or 5th.

## 4. Rhythm
- [I] Mostly quarters, eighths and long notes; eighth triplets as the blues value; 16ths/sextuplets only near the peak (a sextuplet lick is cited [S]).
- [I] Moderate syncopation, ties over the bar line; double-stop slides across bar lines [S]. [I] Laid back 0-30 ms on held notes (unconfirmed).

## 5. Bends (the core)
- [I] 30-50% of held notes bent.
- Whole step most [S]; half steps and quarter-tone curls on b3 and b7 [S]; 1.5 steps [S]; 2 steps at climaxes [S].
- Two-stage bend: up a whole tone, ease off, on to 2 tones [S]. Bend, hold, bend higher, release [S]. Pre-bend and release, slightly flat rather than sharp [S]. Unison bends [S].
- [I] Slow, singing: up over 1/8-1/4 beat, longer on held notes; releases slower. Weights: whole 1, half 0.5, 1.5 steps 0.3, 2 steps 0.15 (big ones at the peak).

## 6. Vibrato
- Wide, even, from the wrist [S]. [I] about +/-0.3-0.5 semitone at 4-6 Hz; on notes >= 1 beat and bend tops; delayed (straight for the first 20-40% of the note).

## 7. Other techniques
- Legato and slides over heavy picking; grace-note slides into notes [S].
- Double stops: slid 3rds or 4ths [S]; [I] about 5-10% of events.
- Register: low/mid to high, the octave up for the peak [S]; [I] about two octaves in a solo.
- Fast runs rare, short, pentatonic, at the climax [S/I]. Whammy and harmonics occasional (out of scope).

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone (Big Muff, fuzz), long delays, volume swells, Leslie/Uni-Vibe, amp feel, pickup mix, pick dynamics.

## 9. Recipe
1. A scale per chord (Dorian minor or major pentatonic + 2nd); a target chord tone per bar.
2. Three sections: sparse/low, developing/mid, peak/high; density about 3 -> 10 onsets a bar; register up about an octave.
3. A 1-2 bar motif: few notes, a pickup or off-beat start, a long last note on a chord tone.
4. Answer it (same rhythm, new ending) after 1-3 beats' rest; repeat the pair higher with one note changed.
5. Bends on long notes: mostly whole steps, b3 curls, a two-stage or 2-step bend at the peak, an occasional pre-bend and release.
6. Delayed, wide, slow vibrato on every note >= 1 beat.
7. One short 16th or sextuplet burst near the peak, back to a long bent note.
8. End on the root or 5th, held with vibrato, after a slow release.
