# Mick Mars: lead style (technique sheet)

Researched for the studio's solo engine. Techniques and habits only; no solo, riff or melody of his is written out here.
**[S]** = sourced (named), **[I]** = inference. **The evidence is thin.** Mars has said a lot about what he values (melody, feel, the blues, not speed) and very little about how he plays it, and no lesson found here analyses his lead playing in detail. The LickLibrary pages are sales descriptions of song lessons. Scale choice, notes per bar, bend sizes, vibrato speed and how often things happen are in no source and stay [I].

## 1. Note choice
- A blues player at heart: "I love the blues", "That's what I love, that's what I listen to" [S, MR 2008]; he "really considers himself a bluesman at heart" [S, MR Blues 10]. Many of his solos "draw heavily from the blues", and he calls that "the more melodic side of me", put "into one box" with his other influences [S, GW 2023].
- Deliberately not scale-based: "Everybody and their mother was playing all scales... everybody's playing the same lick over and over"; "because I didn't play all the scales... people thought that I was this crap guitar player" [S, GW 80s book]; the '80s players "were doing these scales, but there wasn't any feeling at all" [S, MR Blues 10]. He doesn't read music; it came from "following the melody of the song" [S, GW 80s book].
- Pentatonic and "bluesy pentatonic licks" in the song lessons [S, LL GGG, LL DGAM, LL SOS, LL Kickstart] (lesson descriptions, weak evidence).
- [I] Minor pentatonic with the blues scale's b5 as passing colour; over major-key songs (his ballads, the poppier singles) major pentatonic and the major 3rd against the minor 3rd, as Chicago/Texas blues players do. Chord tones on held notes, because he follows the song's melody (above).

## 2. Density and space
- "I'm not really about playing fast, I'm all about the tone and melodies" [S, GW 2023]; "Guitar playing isn't about how fast you can make your fingers go" [S, MR 2008]; "a melody line to it, not just a barrage of notes" [S, GW 80s book].
- But speed now and then: "every now and then, it's okay to stick in a fast idea like that. As long as it fits the song"; the interviewer names a single-string alternate-picking burst [S, GW 2023]. Johnny Winter "taught me speed... a whole different way of approaching how to play fast" than a scale player's [S, MR Blues 10]. He contrasts "a more melodic thing" with "the wheedle-wee stuff" [S, GW Health Kick].
- His pacing: "while others were looking to play faster, Mars dialed it back" (a writer's view of one solo) [S, GW 10 best].
- [I] 3-6 notes a bar in melodic bars; one fast burst (12-16 notes a bar, 16ths or sextuplets) at most once every 8 bars, usually near the end; rests of 1-2 beats between phrases; solos short, 8-16 bars, since they serve the song.

## 3. Phrasing
- Hummable is the goal: "I wanted people to hum my solos, a bit like Jeff Beck... He would leave you with ideas that would stick in your head" [S, GW 2023]; Clapton "plays hooks. That's why you can hum what he does" [S, MR Blues 10]; Keith Nelson: "You could sing the solos" [S, GW Two for the Road].
- Song first: he's "more interested in what the song calls for" (the interviewer; Mars: "That's exactly it") [S, GW 2023]; "Playing something that fit and something that was memorable" [S, GW 80s book].
- Weaving round the singer, from Bloomfield: "how to be melodic... how to fit notes between lyrics", "little bits of solos in and around the singer, weaving the guitar in and out" [S, MR Blues 10].
- Some solos improvised, others worked out for melody [S, GW Health Kick]; quotes of other songs' melodies near a solo's end (a "nod to the classics") [S, GW Health Kick]. [I] Not something to generate.
- Several players' styles in one solo (Page, Winter, Bloomfield in his favourite one) [S, GW Dear Guitar Hero].
- [I] A two-bar vocal-like phrase stated and answered; phrases end on a held chord tone; the solo builds from melodic to a short fast flurry and back to a held note.

## 4. Rhythm
- Nothing sourced about his lead rhythm beyond the occasional fast single-string picking [S, GW 2023] and a "descending staccato run" ending one song (a writer's description) [S, GW Unconscious].
- [I] Blues phrasing: phrases start after the downbeat (beat 1's "and" or beat 2), triplet-feel eighths in shuffles, straight eighths and quarter notes otherwise; held notes cross the bar line. Short staccato notes (half their slot) in fast descending runs.

## 5. Bends
- Bloomfield was "a big influence on me as to the way I learned to bend strings and get everything I could out of a note" [S, GW Dear Guitar Hero]; from Gary Moore "I learned a lot of cool bends" [S, MR Blues 10].
- Bends in the solo lessons [S, LL GGG, LL DGAM, LL SOS] (descriptions only).
- [I] Whole step 60%, half step 25%, 1.5 steps 10%, quarter-tone blues bends on the minor 3rd 5%; a bend held with vibrato is his "everything out of a note" move: about one bend every bar in melodic bars, the bent note held 1-2 beats; bend-and-release in phrase middles.

## 6. Vibrato
- "Aggressive vibrato" (one lesson description) [S, LL Kickstart]; vibrato in another solo's lesson [S, LL SOS].
- [I] Medium-wide (+/-0.4 semitone) at about 5-6 Hz on notes of a beat or more, starting about a quarter into the note; on held bends too.

## 7. Other techniques
- Slide: he played "a lot of slide" (lap steels, Dobros) on one album, though at the band's urging ("I don't wanna play slide") [S, GW Health Kick]; he learned "a lot about slide playing and phrasing" from Muddy Waters and Johnny Winter [S, MR Blues 10]; slide in a song lesson [S, LL GGG].
- Whammy bar as a voice, after Beck: "He used it like a speaking voice or singing voice"; the interviewer calls his use "quite melodic... sometimes heavy gonzo, other times very light" [S, GW 2024]. Floyd Rose dives on a ballad's solo (a guitar swapped in mid-session) [S, GW Two for the Road]; "heavy usage of the whammy bar and harmonics" on one song [S, GW 2023]; pinch harmonics [S, LL Kickstart].
- Blues roots: Butterfield and Bloomfield (Chicago), Winter (Texas), Muddy Waters (Delta), Hendrix, Clapton, Beck, Alvin Lee's jazzy licks [S, MR 2008, MR Blues 10, GW Dear Guitar Hero].
- Sole guitarist: "I like to make fat chords and play stuff over the top" [S, MR 5 Minutes]. [I] Double stops and chord stabs inside solos are plausible but unsourced.
- Talk box [S, GW Health Kick], wah set fixed (Cry Baby Q Zone) [S, GW 2023, GW 2024], octave pedal and Whammy [S, GW 2023]. Out of scope.

## 8. Out of scope for MIDI
Tone ("it's in your hands" [S, GW 2024]), the guitars tuned down a whole step to D [S, GW 2023, GW Health Kick], fixed wah, talk box, octave and Whammy pedals, slide's sound, whammy dives and pitch dips past the bend range, pinch harmonics as sound (a velocity layer at most), doubled and layered tracks, quoting other songs.

## 9. Recipe
1. Minor pentatonic in the key with the b5 as a passing note; in major-key songs, major pentatonic with the minor 3rd bent a quarter tone or slid into the major 3rd [I].
2. Short solo, 8-16 bars; enter after the downbeat with a two-bar vocal-like phrase, 3-6 notes a bar, ending on a held chord tone.
3. Answer the phrase (same rhythm, new ending) so it can be hummed; repeat the pair with a change.
4. Bends: about one a bar, mostly a whole step, the bent note held 1-2 beats with vibrato (Bloomfield's "everything out of a note").
5. Leave 1-2 beats of space between phrases; if the song has vocals around the solo, fill only the gaps between lines.
6. Once, near the end, a fast single-string burst (16ths, 1-2 beats, staccato, descending) and back.
7. Where the profile has a bend range of 2 or more, one whammy-style dip or scoop on a held note (a pitch-bend down and back, "like a voice"), not a dive bomb unless the profile allows it.
8. End on a long held note (a chord tone, often bent up to it) with vibrato to the bar line.

## Notes on the sources
- Mars talks about values (melody, feel, the blues, humming) far more than technique; nearly every number here is [I]. The generator should treat this profile as a melodic blues-rock profile with restraint, more than as a set of signature licks.
- The LickLibrary pages are song-lesson sales copy ("bends, slides, and whammy bar flair", "aggressive vibrato"); they confirm the techniques exist in his solos but say nothing about how often or how big.
- "Mick Mars' 10 best solos" (GW) is a writer's listicle: only its pacing remark is used, and it describes one solo.
- The GW Health Kick slide passage is about one album and he played slide reluctantly; slide is real but not a constant.
- The influences (Bloomfield's bends, Moore's bends, Winter's speed, Waters' slide) are what he says he learned, not analyses of his solos.
- No Guitar Player or Premier Guitar interview with technical detail was found; the Guitar Player Dr. Feelgood piece repeats the Dear Guitar Hero answers about amps.
